# Fork commits point at expert bytes and drafting

After reading the 43,685 fork-only commits of the 407 Strata forks that have their own work (fork knowledge base `data/fork-kb/forks.sqlite`, `upstream_history = 0`, 2026-09-24 to 2026-11-10), the answer is clear. No exact GPU kernel in the forks will make pancake-strata much faster. The ideas that can bring 5% or more all do one of three things. They cut the bytes the CPU pool reads for missed experts. They cut the verify rows that pay for those misses. Or they stop the agent turn from re-reading a prompt it has already read. The first tests need no code at all. Exact speculative sampling with a 0.85 draft temperature (`STRATA_SPEC_PROB=1 STRATA_SPEC_COUPLED=1 STRATA_SPEC_PROB_GATE=top STRATA_SPEC_PROB_DT=0.85`) is already in our binary but has never been measured here. `--prompt-cache-spread`, `--lookup-max-t 16 --lookup-chain 3` and `--aux-cpus auto` are in the same position. On the RTX 3060 presets, `STRATA_EXPDEC=1` and the lossy `STRATA_HC_Q8=1` are two settings that the closest sm_86 lab measured as a loss or as not worth it. After those, the exact ports that pay on **both** GPUs come first. They are the cumulative draft stop (`STRATA_SPEC_CUM`), the fast router publish, the faster bit-exact Q2_0 AVX2 kernel, the Q2_0 down-kernel lane fold (all 48 of our down projections are Q2_0), and partial parking (`STRATA_PARK_TRUNCATE`). Partial parking is the likely fix for one opencode session in our own turn log that re-read a 54-61K conversation from token 0 seven times in a row (about 162 s). The lossy arms come last and go behind the quality gate: the speculative-tail expert cap (`STRATA_VERIFY_ECAP`), guards that would let `turbo` run a stronger route prior without touching tool calls, a sub-2-bit "shadow" store used only for CPU misses, and the ISTA Coder pack. Every speed number in this report is a fork author's claim on the author's hardware, named each time. **Nothing here was built, run or measured on our box.** Our own numbers come from earlier pancake rounds and are marked as such. The exact-only ceiling is modest: about +5-6% from window shaping (estimate) plus a few percent from kernels. Larger gains need fewer expert bytes, and those are lossy.

## Where pancake's decode time goes, and why kernel wins stay small

Every estimate below starts from pancake's measured window anatomy on the RX 7900 XT, preset `flashnext-iq2_xs-fast-hip` (README section 5 and MOD_PLAN Phase 13, measured in earlier pancake rounds). **A short-chat window takes 24.8 ms: 15.9 GPU, 5.2 CPU expert misses, 2.2 MTP draft. A code-edit window takes 37.4 ms: 15.8 GPU, 17.4 CPU experts, 2.5 draft.** A rocprofv3 run on our card (`bench/results/2026-11-09-7900xt-gpuprof/run_kernel_stats.csv`) puts `combine_cpu_k10_vec4` at 38.3% of GPU kernel time. That kernel is the GPU waiting for CPU expert rows. The CPU Q2_0 kernel already runs at about 80% of the 5700G's DDR4 bandwidth. A rule of thumb follows from this. **Cutting CPU expert bytes by X% buys at most about 0.46·X% on edit windows and 0.21·X% on chat windows.** A GPU-kernel saving shows up in tok/s only where the GPU path is the critical one: short chat, or the stretch between a layer's router and the CPU pool's doorbell.

Drafting sets the second ceiling. The owner's opencode traffic (`pancake-turns.jsonl`, 605 turns with draft counters) shows **75.4% draft acceptance, about 2.73 tokens per verify window, and about 0.56 rejected rows per window (17% of verified rows)**. Each extra row mostly costs CPU-missed experts. So a perfect draft stop is worth at most about 5-6% of window time (my estimate). Fork data agree. android-imdad's replay on an RX 7900 XTX finds that even an oracle stop would give +21% on a card that holds about 90% of experts in VRAM, and that the draft probabilities, not the stop rule, are the limit (author, RX 7900 XTX, Flash-Next IQ3_S) ([android-imdad f1bd09212a](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/f1bd09212adbdf431b393b54d527b8bffefea96c)).

The RTX 3060 sits further toward the CPU end of this picture. Pancake measured 75 tok/s with 4,080 experts in VRAM on the 3060, against 125-129 tok/s with 10,909 on the 7900 XT (earlier pancake rounds, memory file). On the 3060 the CPU bucket is larger, so CPU-side and drafting gains are worth relatively more. Its dense chain is also slower (360 GB/s against 936 GB/s on the 3090 that most sm_86 fork data come from), so hyper-connection (HC) and dense-projection kernels weigh more there as well. **The 3060 is out of the box right now. Every 3060-only item waits for the card to come back.**

### CPU expert bytes: the only lever with double-digit headroom is lossy

The strongest new idea in this area is svgop's **shadow expert store** (`STRATA_SHADOW_EXPERTS`). Only the experts that miss the GPU cache are computed from a lighter, mapped Q2_0 copy. Hits, the PCIe share, the prompt path and every combine stay on the native pack ([svgop 25bf1b90f6](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/25bf1b90f645dd5a44344bb4d04c7042805db95f)). svgop called the CPU pool "byte-bound" and measured +2.9% solo and **+20.8% under concurrency** (author, RTX 5090 box, IQ3_S Flash-Next) ([svgop 5c46e95b23](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/5c46e95b231a791d3aca77c43538952f2e1d3da2)). Moving the store to tmpfs or a hugetlb memfd added another +5.5% solo by removing about 2.6 M minor faults per run (author, same box) ([svgop 9e889bda3a](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/9e889bda3a838ccf932a25cbd88d6430742beb6e), [8aa066b17d](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/8aa066b17d5cd5b75aac570261568e6e6b2fe283)). For pancake a Q2_0 shadow is nearly the same thing as switching to the Q2_0 file, which we already did (+5-9% on writing, measured earlier on our box). So the real candidate is a **sub-2-bit shadow (IQ1_M gate/up, about -15% to -22% bytes against Q2_0, by block-size arithmetic) used for misses only**. My estimate is edit +7-10% and chat +3-5% if the pool stays byte-bound. The quality cost is unknown: gputier measured KL 0.47 for whole-model IQ2_XXS experts and dropped it (author, Ornith) ([gputier 67d5ff661f](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/67d5ff661f)). It also needs an imatrix requant of about 25-30 GB on a nearly full disk. mandoof1's `STRATA_ALT_EXPERTS` is the same idea aimed at the page-cache tier ([85b991da79](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/85b991da79303fcd018bcc90f1a763067c93fa1e)). That tier does not exist for us, since the arena sits fully in RAM.

The **ISTA Coder pack (256 of 512 experts)**, MOD_PLAN item 25, halves the routed working set. svgop's masked-router analogue on DeepSeek gave **+7.8% solo, +10.0% pair, +11.1% concurrent** (author, RTX 5090) ([svgop 9e309adb39](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/9e309adb39b7d71c7e7279035b0320bd16a6e65f)). svgop deleted it the same day because the quality trade "buys nothing over the base" ([93b85b38e7](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/93b85b38e7)). Our tier hit rate is 79.6% at 44% residency, much lower than svgop's 97.7%, so the relative gain should be larger here. That is an inference, and the Coder's bits per weight are reported inconsistently (VlDubr [f07c68c7c2](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/f07c68c7c2)).

The exact items in this area are small but cheap. imanu86's **bit-exact Q2_0 AVX2 kernel** unpacks codes in 8 vector ops instead of about 17 and processes 4 rows together. The author measured 1.11-1.45x on one thread and "gate/up Q2_0 -8%, all-Q2_0 -17% per layer" in the real 7-worker pool (author, desktop CPU, IQ3_S/Q2_0 model; `STRATA_Q2_LEGACY=1` restores the old path) ([imanu86 c3fc8f34d3](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/c3fc8f34d37bfcfe3855f21d532f49c3d861771a)). Our `q2_avx2.cpp` still has the 14-op `unpack64`. Every one of our down projections is Q2_0, so this matters for both files. My estimate is edit +1-3% and chat +0.5-1%. zsariboga's `STRATA_ADAPT_PROMPT` (about 10 lines) adds a prompt's routing counts to the adaptive tier so that the first decode adaptation fits the new prompt ([4ce19344ff](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/4ce19344ff72c8ca9a1175a01d357c1179a521e8)). A counter-measurement exists: RandoTeam moved the cache *during* the prompt and read prompts 3% slower (author, RTX 5090, GLM) ([1eabf41cbc](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/1eabf41cbc53eb03a558fa3b7ec75727ee929f1a)). ADAPT_PROMPT only adds counts, so the swaps still happen in decode. tctien342's `--light-expert-cpu` weights small blobs toward the CPU ([5576b48144](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/commit/5576b481449c638517c1835c15bb053bd1443ab5)). It is worth 0-2% at most, and only on the mixed-format IQ2_XS file.

### Verify rows and drafting: two env tests, one exact port, one lossy cap

The cheapest acceptance lever is already compiled in. **Exact speculative sampling** (the drafter samples q and the verifier accepts with min(1, p/q)) gave **+5.5% on agent turns at temperature 1.0** (author, Intel B70) ([riceharvest 7389a10f2c](https://github.com/riceharvest/strata-b70-only/commit/7389a10f2c093e121db0618fe69f3bc8d7f9d253)). It gave +5.8% (author, RTX 5090) ([svgop 0355c3164f](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/0355c3164f8d2ec5e73a0cb4a438d07101fd3040)), but an earlier svgop run called it neutral ([65e4bdae03](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/65e4bdae03e07ce16a6f9cd12ec58df4e84069ca)). A **0.85 draft temperature** added up to **+13.4%** (author, RTX 5090, IQ3_S) ([svgop b59c0e6bbc](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/b59c0e6bbc35d37e1bc6cc6495e020f2582f9584)). Our switch for that is `STRATA_SPEC_PROB_DT`. Pancake already meets riceharvest's exactness condition (`--pcie-frac 0`, no `STRATA_SPEC_COST`). Upstream's own note in `spec_prob.hpp` says sampled drafting loses below about 0.7 temperature. The gain is therefore 0 on greedy turns and an estimated +3-8% on sampled ones, and the share of sampled opencode traffic has to be checked first.

**`STRATA_SPEC_CUM`** keeps a draft only while the product of draft probabilities stays at or above S. The drafter stops at the same point. The author measured +1.8% with pinned text, and **+6.4% short chat and +2.1% agent sessions** with `--lookup-chain 3` (author, RX 7900 XTX, IQ3_S) ([android-imdad f1bd09212a](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/f1bd09212adbdf431b393b54d527b8bffefea96c), [4fe0492f53](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/4fe0492f53f428e8b58bfc99aca318ccb4cc5ca6)). Its lookup-agreement arm `STRATA_SPEC_LOOKUP_Q` won +5.8% on unseen prompts. It was "a wash" once suffix drafts were on, which is our default ([ef42c2fde9](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/ef42c2fde9313e70a5204dab79bdc850dc686fd0), [0633cd52c9](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/0633cd52c95798ddbe6ff0bd03d14ed8e1456cbc)). The port is exact and about 150 lines. I estimate +1-3%, more on edit windows. It is the runtime twin of our offline `tools/pancake/trace/tail_replay.py`, so the replay should decide it first. On the same card android-imdad also found `--spec 6` +5-20% faster than `--spec 4` ([f9fac49321](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/f9fac49321a25a5ef4acc4ffd072b87b2a90aef2)). That contradicts our flat `--spec 5/6` result and fits the row-cost picture. Once SPEC_CUM exists, SPEC_CUM plus `--spec 6` is a cheap two-arm test.

**`STRATA_VERIFY_ECAP=N`** is the one lossy item aimed squarely at our dominant cost. Token 0 keeps all ten experts. The speculative tail keeps only its top entries by routing weight, and dropped entries are redirected at weight 0, so no expert outside the kept set is read ([jmnargi 24894e676b](https://github.com/jmnargi/Strata-V100/commit/24894e676b1de0364b388cafd347702262885559)). The author reports **+11.5% in drift-paired runs but only +2.8% on pooled medians** (author, 2x V100-SXM2, 32K) ([7c5041c358](https://github.com/jmnargi/Strata-V100/commit/7c5041c3584276863faef21c1cff19d6a5b9c256)). A cap that was too tight **ended 12 of 20 tool-calling turns with truncated reasoning and no call** ([376355453f](https://github.com/jmnargi/Strata-V100/commit/376355453f68d1776c55df034ba8c5e2866a23cf)). That is exactly our workload, so the tool-call gate is mandatory. My estimate on edits is +3-10%, in the same substitute group as `STRATA_VERIFY_DEFER_CPU` and turbo.

Lookup drafting already carries most of the edit-turn gain. zsariboga saw **1,477 of 1,487 lookup drafts accepted** on a rename-style edit, where the window cap was the limit (author, RTX 2060S) ([712707942d](https://github.com/zsariboga/Strata/commit/712707942da9401c0fa1c083de7af552f8a4f4f9)). That makes `--lookup-max-t 16` (default 12) and `--lookup-chain 3` (ours is 0) free tests, estimated at 0-4% on edits. mandoof1's `STRATA_DRAFT_EARLY` launches the next draft before commit and emit, and `STRATA_DRAFT_SUFFIX_SKIP` stops a chain whose next window will be a lookup window ([b8004eb8d7](https://github.com/mandoof1/Strata/commit/b8004eb8d758869d378441f5d371445bb3e386db), [868841cb32](https://github.com/mandoof1/Strata/commit/868841cb32d16dc9a1ce160a4aee6b3224254bdc)). Neither has a published number. The draft step is 2.2-2.5 ms, which caps both at about 1%. tctien342's shared cross-request n-gram pool (`STRATA_NGRAM_MOD`) accepted 234 of 234 drafts on a copy fixture, with no tok/s A/B ([05c5a841e9](https://github.com/tctien342/strata-x/commit/05c5a841e910405b8e1e91f04ee32a99b2249a01)). A trace replay should come before any port.

### Agent turns: one cold-reread bug is worth more than any kernel

Our own turn log (622 turns, read-only analysis, earlier pancake data) shows that warm turns are cheap: `prompt_ms ≈ 226 + 0.912 × tokens read`. It also shows **7 consecutive turns at 54,313-61,565 tokens with zero reuse, 21.2-25.0 s each (about 162 s)**, sitting between warm turns at 53K and 62K. The engine log says the conversation cache was sized at 4,864 MiB for up to 4 parked conversations. That matches TwoAnts' symptom: parking refused the whole snapshot when the chain did not fit, so the next request re-read from token 0 (225 s for a 160K chain). TwoAnts' fix `STRATA_PARK_TRUNCATE=1` parks the deepest checkpoint prefix that fits ([TwoAnts 5ef4fc8b4b](https://github.com/TwoAnts/Strata/commit/5ef4fc8b4b8a97848371ebf76fdcd5e46f710b33)). It is exact. A log grep must confirm the mechanism first, but if confirmed, **20+ s per affected turn** dwarfs any kernel change.

Three exact cache items follow. `--prompt-cache-spread` is already in our binary but not in the preset. It resumed an edited 82K conversation in **10.2 s instead of 40.0 s** (author, 2x Turing) ([xrip 56017b1c41](https://github.com/xrip/Strata/commit/56017b1c414cb6f66e271741a4cbb97a32cdb31a)), and Lamina saw re-read cells drop from 130,206 to 86,218 ([13d90a38ec](https://github.com/eddoursul/Lamina/commit/13d90a38ec9a8e443af21e458833b79ef1cb0221)). `STRATA_PARK_REWIND_MAX` and `--conversation-cache-skip-tail` skip a park when the request rewinds its own tail. They took 12 appended turns from 10.4 s to 6.3 s of prompt reading (author, i7-13700K + RTX 5090) ([Xiaomi2008 8e4ccbe90d](https://github.com/Xiaomi2008/Strata/commit/8e4ccbe90d2e8dbab5c8ddf7f19d2c8159dc33d8)) and cut 5 parks to 1 (author, gfx1101, Flash-Next Q2_0) ([UltimateBoomer 5d0a9e786c](https://github.com/UltimateBoomer/Strata/commit/5d0a9e786c5d275b3f1f8bb1a48e95a20094b918)). Lamina's fork-point checkpoint cut 6 agent tasks from 412 s to 369 s (author, RTX 3060, IQ3_S) ([ab808b133e](https://github.com/eddoursul/Lamina/commit/ab808b133e0dfba74bddc9742296f14e7ccb30b7)). Its borrow-before-newest-checkpoint cut a sub-agent switch from 3.00 s to 0.67 s (author, same class of box) ([83f40aee62](https://github.com/eddoursul/Lamina/commit/83f40aee62128e6031ae0c18ae5cd220d8d397f7)). Ben1078's trailing-reminder move matters only if opencode appends reminder-only turns ([8b90ab80](https://github.com/Ben1078/Strata/commit/8b90ab8077ed943d7b2f0e3e31417608062885e2)). All of these are backend-agnostic. For parallel sub-agents, `--batch 2` is config-only. Concurrency gave 70/105/150 tok/s aggregate at 2/4/8 requests (author, 2x RX 7900 XTX) ([unimatrix099 fa10a01bd3](https://github.com/unimatrix099/Strata/commit/fa10a01bd3bddaa0684acbca43e3a72b86ca82a9)). On a 20 GB card it competes with the expert cache.

### GPU decode floor: exact, small, and better on the GPU that waits less

The GPU items compete for the 15.9 ms GPU floor. That floor pays fully on short chat. On edits it pays only after the CPU bucket shrinks. The best HIP item is the **router publish fast path**. Our `route_publish_res` averages **17.8 µs over 4,128 calls (2.68% of GPU time)** in our own profile. It copies every MoE input row to host memory and then does a read-modify-write of the step counter across PCIe. unimatrix099 timed the top-10 alone at 3.5 µs per layer (author, RX 7900 XTX) ([5255b00e1a](https://github.com/unimatrix099/Strata/commit/5255b00e1acf1d74079558b4954fa050a2023de6)), so most of our 17.8 µs is the copy and the round trip. android-imdad's `STRATA_ROUTE_FAST` stores the step number, moves the row copy into the router GEMV and uses DPP lane exchanges ("~1 µs less a launch" for DPP alone, author, RX 7900 XTX) ([caa9b00115](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/commit/caa9b00115e7f134292cc5be971e35095934f587)). Ben1078 has the same store-not-RMW doorbell ([23d2fc0450](https://github.com/Ben1078/Strata/commit/23d2fc0450a642d984e64724b4925cabc202836c)). On CUDA, Lamina's router gives the mapped copy its own blocks, uses one fence round and a `__reduce_max_sync` top-10, for **+2.5% code and +2.7% after 16K** (author, RTX 3090, IQ3_XXS) ([Lamina f14591215f](https://github.com/eddoursul/Lamina/commit/f14591215f01afb8c0cf188f5800aba82d5fa9fc)). My estimate is +1-2% on both GPUs. One caution comes first: pancake already has `DOORBELL_STORE` in `verify.cpp`, so a probe has to confirm which path the HIP preset really runs.

The **Q2_0 down kernel** is our other shared target. KhaledElzarw's `STRATA_EXP9_DOWN=42:10` folds the first two butterfly levels into in-lane adds on 4 lanes. Down time fell to 0.47-0.96x of mode 8's, for **+1.6-2.0% end to end in a paired 57-turn A/B** (author, RTX 3090, Flash-Next IQ3_XXS, bitwise on 30 Q2_0 layers) ([4d462ccff3](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/commit/4d462ccff3e46308ea684ada1a3888e1cc2cd716)). We have Q2_0 downs in **all 48 layers**, and `native_down_multi_kernel<42>` is 5.79% of our gfx1100 kernel time. Lamina's `native_down_sub_kernel` is the same idea, and its sub-warp gate/up kernels gave +5.7-8.3% end to end (author, RTX 3090 + 5070 Ti, IQ3_S) ([674bf0caf2](https://github.com/eddoursul/Lamina/commit/674bf0caf236b9276bd82a73b3e6fb948691a6d8)). My estimate is +1-3% on the 3060 and 0-1.5% on the 7900 XT. It should be measured against our never-measured `STRATA_EXPDEC_DOWN`.

The rest of the GPU list is backend-specific. For the 3060, KhaledElzarw's lab forks **the exact upstream commit pancake is based on (`146df3c46`)**. Its bitwise HC read kernels (`GR_DOWN82/86`, `UP_HYB`, `XN`, `UP_PL`, `D87`) gave +2.3% plus part of a +4.7% "tier 5" step. The 3090-measured MMVQ_IL tables gave +1.8%. In total, **+17.3% over its phase-1 config and +19.9% over stock**, with 95% intervals over 57 agent turns (author, RTX 3090, Ryzen 9 9950X, DDR5) ([README](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata), [857158c66d](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/commit/857158c66dd59298dae78aaa8b2fecb7bcae63ab), [20f1fa1a95](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/commit/20f1fa1a9504571676ba049a9a8f2414c87454c7), [ca1edf8786](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/commit/ca1edf87869ff1113527d2ec18f93a3c4d7c6c3e)). The same lab measured `STRATA_EXPDEC=1` **3.0% slower**, and `STRATA_HC_Q8=1` +0.9%, "not worth its KL of 0.025" ([NOTES](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/blob/main/sm86/NOTES.md)). Both are set in our 3060 presets. For the 7900 XT there are four items. First, a **fill-stall audit**: a 384x256 launch, exactly 48 WGPs x 8 blocks, cost 14-16 µs even when empty, and trimming one copy kernel to 96 blocks gave +6% decode (author, 2x RX 7900 XTX) ([unimatrix099 d0c8d6c3e2](https://github.com/unimatrix099/Strata/commit/d0c8d6c3e2c1607447ff342b2aa1fd59c385b1b1)). Upstream's `grid_pad` (PR #2492) fixes this on gfx12 only ([mandoof1 2b46e4b13d](https://github.com/mandoof1/Strata/commit/2b46e4b13d80fa4dc27893a36a63d81e67eedb86)). Our daily preset already avoids the one known case, because `--pcie-frac 0` skips `fetch_blobs`. Second, the **IQ2 grid-sign expansion trick**, measured on an RX 7900 XT itself: IQ2_XXS gate/up -29%, IQ2_S down -35% per kernel (author, GLM engine, ROCm 7.2.1) ([yqYo1 6f0bdf665b](https://github.com/yqYo1/Strata/commit/6f0bdf665b4c01a24d086210a2506beae3f87b56)). There is a drop-in version for our `iq_kernels.cu` that may lose waves ([RX5950XT b9a2111d3f](https://github.com/RX5950XT/Strata/commit/b9a2111d3f4ece53487ce054e1b23aa3a8939214)). Third, `STRATA_SH_STREAM=1`, which gave +4.0% on gfx1100 (author, RX 7900 XTX, IQ3_S) ([xyzzing 320e4324a8](https://github.com/xyzzing/Strata/commit/320e4324a86e1c2e3bbe7fe4f5b586ac878ea446)) but was noise on a 3060 (author, RTX 3060) ([AdrianBM96 82c9640da5](https://github.com/AdrianBM96/Strata3060/commit/82c9640da5dfb4848a378843539dc691684e9c30)). ## Cross-backend portability (HIP <-> CUDA)

A port is never mechanical. gfx1100 runs wave32, so CUDA warp intrinsics (`__shfl_xor_sync`, `__reduce_max_sync`) become DPP row_xmask, `v_permlanex16` or ds_swizzle. Tensor-core `mma.sync` becomes `v_wmma_*_16x16x16`. RDNA3 has int8 WMMA (`v_wmma_i32_16x16x16_iu8`), but it runs at the same rate as FP16 WMMA, not at Ampere's doubled int8 rate. An int8 path on gfx1100 therefore saves bytes, not math. `cp.async` rings have no HIP equivalent and become plain LDS staging. Launch cost is the other big difference. On gfx1100 a dependent kernel in a HIP graph costs about 2.8 µs (author, RX 7900 XTX) ([android-imdad doc](https://github.com/android-imdad/Strata-7900xtx/blob/rx7900xtx/docs/RX7900XTX.md)), and a fill-stall launch costs 14-16 µs. A CUDA graph on a 3090 loses only 0.3 ms per window to launch gaps (author) ([KhaledElzarw NOTES](https://github.com/KhaledElzarw/Strata/blob/main/sm86/NOTES.md)). So **fusions and grid trimming are worth more on the 7900 XT, while dense-chain kernel speed is worth more on the 3060**. ## Learn faster by testing

The test order is built so that each run answers the largest open question per minute of GPU time, and so that no port is written before a probe says it can pay. There are four kinds of tests. Some need no GPU and can run on the CPU while another session holds the GPU (they still need a quiet CPU if they time CPU work). Some are microbenchmarks that price a port before anyone writes it. Some are env A/Bs where one run answers a question for both backends. The rest are full A/Bs whose results do not transfer and must be re-measured on the other card.

### (a) Env A/Bs that answer for both backends in one run

Some results do not depend on the GPU at all, so one run on the 7900 XT answers the question for the 3060 too. They depend only on the model, the drafter, the CPU or the server. Draft acceptance under SPEC_PROB and DT is a property of the model and the MTP head. Lookup acceptance and the cache and park behaviour are properties of the traffic and the server. Pool and helper-thread placement depends on the 5700G, which both boxes share. So FK-01 (SPEC_PROB + DT), FK-02 (prompt-cache-spread), FK-03 (lookup 16 / chain 3), FK-04 (aux-cpus) and every CPU-kernel port (FK-23) are measured once on the 7900 XT. Their **acceptance and per-turn numbers transfer**. Their **tok/s value does not transfer one for one**: the 3060 pays more CPU-missed experts per verify row, so the same acceptance gain is worth relatively more there. Treat the 7900 XT tok/s as a lower bound for the 3060.

### (d) What predicts the other GPU, and what must be re-measured

Lossy routing and verify arms (ECAP, route-prior guards, shadow store) change the model's arithmetic, not a kernel. Their quality-gate result (token forcing, tool-call gate) on one card is therefore a strong predictor for the other. A spot check is still needed, because numerics differ slightly between backends and svgop showed that cross-boot greedy text is not a bitwise instrument ([svgop 2a9d335d43](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/2a9d335d430ea8da435b0bf4739ad77e75ed40f7)). Everything below must be re-measured per card: any GPU kernel timing, grid and launch shape, `--pcie-frac` (0 won on the 7900 XT, but the 3060 has 4,080 slots and more misses), `SH_STREAM` (+4% on gfx1100 against noise on a 3060, both author claims), `STRATA_EXPDEC`, `STRATA_HC_Q8`, `--vram-reserve-mib`, prefill chunk size (it trades against expert slots, and the two cards have different VRAM) and `GPU_MAX_HW_QUEUES`. One more prediction carries over: an edit-window gain measured on the 7900 XT from a CPU-side change is a lower bound for the same change on the 3060.

### The order that teaches the most

The best order is: offline O1-O9 now, while the GPU is busy. Then one GPU session of probes P1-P4 and P7 (about 2 hours). Then the both-backend env batch (FK-01 to FK-04, about 2 hours with ABBA x 3). Then the HIP-only env arms (FK-07 to FK-10). Ports follow, in the order the probes allow. When the 3060 returns, it starts with P5 and P6 and the 3060 env batch (FK-05, FK-06, FK-11). Only then do the KhaledElzarw kernels go on the 3060.

## The prioritized plan: what goes into MOD_PLAN.md and what into bench/TEST_PLAN.md

The split follows the repo's rules. **bench/TEST_PLAN.md** gets everything that is a measurement: env-only arms, probes, offline replays, and the A/B arm of every port once it is coded. **MOD_PLAN.md** gets every item that needs code or a new artifact (port, requant, model download), with the usual line format, a substitute-group tag and the TEST_PLAN spec it waits on. Env-only items get a one-line pointer in MOD_PLAN under the matching substitute group (S1 CPU experts, S3 window, S5 re-reads, S7 GPU floor) and no code item. Inside each phase, items useful on both GPUs come before single-GPU items, then larger expected gains. Lossy items always run `tools/pancake/quality_gate.py` and the tool-call check. ECAP also needs jmnargi's guard N ≥ K + max_t − 1.

## Conclusion

Most of the cheap value is in switches we already compiled and never measured, and in one cache bug our own log already shows. The fork data also refine a point of method. The forks that won big on decode (android-imdad +18%, KhaledElzarw +19.9%, both author claims) got there by stacking many measured 1-6% steps with intervals. Neither found a single trick. The steps that transfer to us are the ones that are backend-agnostic, or that attack the bytes behind our CPU misses. So pancake's leverage per hour is highest in host, CPU and server code, and in the lossy byte cutters. HIP kernel work comes later, as a short-chat polish. A second, quieter lesson is about measurement. Several forks contradicted themselves between runs: svgop on SPEC_PROB, rxr2 and UltimateBoomer on the same gfx11 switches. An A/B without ABBA order and intervals is not evidence. The offline replays and probes in this plan exist so that the GPU sessions only confirm what the traces already predict.
