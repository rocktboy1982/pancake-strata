## MoE-Lightning (arXiv 2411.11217)

### Takeaway
VERIFIED as text. The "up to 10.3x" figure is "without request padding" and "up to 3.5x" is "with request padding", both as stated in the abstract; the body gives 10.3x against FlexGen on MTBench under S1 and S2. Table 4 (HELM, S1 and S2) does not reproduce either headline: its largest padded ratio over the best baseline is 2.10x. The notes' "on one 16 GB T4" is too narrow: S2 is an L4.

### Cited Findings
- Plan/notes number: "up to 10.3x" (notes, "without padding", on one T4). Paper, abstract: "up to 10.3× (without request padding) and 3.5× (with request padding) higher throughput over state-of-the-art systems on a single GPU" — [MoE-Lightning arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11217), PDF p.1 and conclusion. VERIFIED for the wording; the notes' "over the best baseline" matches the abstract's "best of the existing systems".
- The 10.3x in the body: "MoE-Lightning achieves up to 10.3× better throughput compared with the [FlexGen]" in the MTBench end-to-end (Figure 7, settings S1 and S2) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11217), Figure 7 caption and baseline paragraph. The figure values are not in the text layer, so the MTBench numbers themselves were NOT checked.
- The 3.5x in the body: "On a single GPU (S1 and S2), MoE-Lightning (p) achieves up to 3.5×, 5×, and 6.7× improvement over FlexGen, FlexGen(c), and DeepSpeed" — HELM paragraph of the results, [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11217). Table 4 does not show this. Table 4 (HELM, S1 and S2, tokens/s), with MoE-Lightning(p) over the best baseline:
  - Synthetic Reasoning S1 (1x T4 16 GB): best baseline FlexGen 22.691; MoE-Lightning(p) 26.349; ratio 1.16x.
  - Synthetic Reasoning S2 (1x L4 24 GB): best baseline FlexGen 50.138; MoE-Lightning(p) 105.29; ratio 2.10x.
  - Summarization S1: FlexGen 3.868; MoE-Lightning(p) 4.52; ratio 1.17x.
  - Summarization S2: FlexGen 7.14; MoE-Lightning(p) 12.393; ratio 1.74x.
  So the largest ratio in Table 4 is 2.10x, not 3.5x. The 3.5x is stated in the text but is not in the table I could read.
- Setup: Mixtral 8x7B, single T4 16 GB (S1) and single L4 24 GB (S2), 2.2-2.3 GHz, 24-core host, 192 GB RAM (S1-S8 configuration table, [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11217)). Baselines: FlexGen, FlexGen(c), DeepSpeed Zero-Inference. Throughput is generation throughput in tokens/s.
- Exact or lossy: the paper does not claim numerical identity with baselines; CPU attention is in float32 (notes, full text read; not re-checked in this pass).

### Inferences
- The 10.3x and 3.5x headlines are not comparable with each other: the first is unpadded MTBench, the second is padded. Anyone quoting one of them should say which.
- Table 4 numbers are the only ones in the paper that a reader can recompute from text. They support a 1.2x to 2.1x gain for the padded variant on HELM.

### Gaps
- MTBench figure values (where 10.3x comes from) are in Figure 7 as an image; not read.
- Where the 3.5x figure comes from in the body (a figure or a prompt-length sweep) is not identified.

## OSDI '26 stream-loading prefill (arXiv 2606.10493), plan A32

### Takeaway
Headline numbers VERIFIED: stream-loading prefill (SLP) gives 1,200 tokens/s on one node (32K prompts within 30 s) and distributed SLP gives 1,800 tokens/s on two RTX 5090s (45K prompts within 30 s). The paper's own link is PCIe 5.0 x16, so the plan's "PCIe 3.0 caps it" is not from this paper. The plan's "exact" label is not supported: the paper claims original-precision models (no quantization) but I found no bit-level equivalence claim.

### Cited Findings
- Plan number: "about 1,200 and 1,800 tokens/s" (ROUND2_REPORT, notes). Paper, abstract and intro: SLP "boosting prefill throughput to 1,200 tokens/s and enabling 32K prompts within 30 seconds"; DSLP "reaching 1,800 tokens/s and 45K prompts in 30 seconds on two RTX 5090s" — [arXiv 2606.10493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10493), abstract and intro paragraph (text p.2 and p.2 bullets). VERIFIED.
- Plan/notes: decode for Q4_K_M DeepSeek-R1 drops from 28 tok/s (1K-8K) to 19 tok/s at 128K; KTransformers drops from 22 to 15 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10493), decode comparison paragraph (Figure 17, Q4_K_M DeepSeek-R1). VERIFIED: "our engine ... sustains 28 tokens/s" at 1K-8K, "maintaining 19 tokens/s at the maximum length" (128K); KTransformers "around 15 tokens/s at 128K" and 22 at short lengths. These decode numbers are for the Q4_K_M comparison, not the FP8 engine.
- Plan A32 row: "PCIe 3.0 caps it". DISCREPANT with the paper's setup: "Although weights still necessarily traverse the CPU-GPU path over a PCIe 5.0 x16 link" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10493), system design section. The PCIe 3.0 cap in the plan is a local assumption, not a source number.
- Plan A32 row: "exact". NOT SUPPORTED by the paper text I read. The paper's wording is "intact, original-precision inference" (intro) and it reports quality as official scores, not as bitwise equality. The notes' "within about 1 to 1.2 points of official scores; not bit-exact" was NOT located in my text pass (no "bit" phrase found; the quality sentence was not searched for by number).
- Plan A32 row: "TTFT at 32K+ only". Consistent with the paper: "At small prefill lengths (256-2K), the CPU baselines are competitive"; "For short contexts (4K), weight transfer dominates SLP and DSLP latency, thus no dominant advantage over KT AMX"; "we use the AVX FP8 kernel for requests up to 4K tokens and switch to SLP/DSLP for longer contexts" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10493), Figure 13 discussion (prefill section).
- Setup: 2x RTX 5090 (32 GB each), 2x EPYC 9355, PCIe 5.0 x16; DeepSeek-V3/R1 and Kimi-K2 class models (notes, Sec. 4.1, not re-read in this pass). The model behind the 1,200 tokens/s headline is not named in the abstract text I read.

### Inferences
- For our box, the paper's 1,200 tokens/s is not a number our hardware can be expected to reach; it depends on the 5090 pair and PCIe 5.0.
- The paper's own SLP vs AVX gain at 20-32K is "more than an order of magnitude" (text), and vs estimated KT AMX 2.8x, and DSLP adds 1.64x over SLP.

### Gaps
- The model for the 1,200 tokens/s headline (not named in abstract/intro).
- A figure-level read of Figure 13 (the actual prefill bars) was not done; numbers above are from text.

## Leviathan et al., speculative decoding (arXiv 2211.17192), plan A21 and A56

### Takeaway
VERIFIED. The 2X-3X latency improvement is for T5-XXL (11B) against the T5X implementation at batch size 1 on one TPU-v4, and the body reports 2.3X-3.4X. The acceptance rule and expected-acceptance formula used by A56 are in the paper (Section 3).

### Cited Findings
- Plan A21 "2-3x on T5-XXL (paper, different regime)". Abstract and intro: "showing an out-of-the-box latency improvement of 2X-3X, without any change to the outputs" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17192), intro paragraph (T5X comparison). VERIFIED.
- Body, Table 2 / experiments section: translation speedups 2.6X (temp=1) and 3.4X (temp=0); summarization 2.3X (temp=1) and 3.1X (temp=0) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17192), experiments section. Hardware: "batch size of 1 on a single TPU-v4 for both argmax sampling" (experimental setup).
- Plan A56 (expected acceptance rate, per-position min(1, p/q)): acceptance rule "draft token x ~ q is kept with probability min(1, p(x)/q(x))" and the expectation alpha = E[min(p, q)] (Corollary 3.6, "α = 1 − E(DLK(p, q)) = E(min(p, q))") — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.17192), Section 3. VERIFIED as a formula.
- Exact: "without changing the distribution" (sampled path). The output-identity claim is for argmax sampling in the experiments. A21 is therefore "exact in distribution" for sampled requests.

### Inferences
- The "2-3x" in the plan is the paper's own headline; the body gives up to 3.4x at temperature 0. Either is a T5-XXL on TPU-v4 result, not a 7B-class MoE on a GPU.

### Gaps
- No Qwen or MoE-class target in this paper; the 2-3x does not transfer to our target directly.

## PRESERVE (arXiv 2501.08192), plan A35

### Takeaway
1.6x VERIFIED (1.61x on Llama3-8B). DISCREPANT mapping: the paper prefetches weights into the on-chip L2 cache of Huawei Ascend 910B NPUs in a distributed (tensor-parallel) setting. The plan's "warm the 80 MiB Infinity Cache of an AMD GPU" is our own mapping, and the 80 MiB figure is not in the paper.

### Cited Findings
- Plan A35 row: "PRESERVE, Huawei Zurich 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08192" as the source of the Infinity Cache warming idea. Abstract: "up to 1.6× end-to-end speedup on state-of-the-art, open-source LLMs" and "a further 1.25× improvement in performance per cost by selecting the optimal L2 cache size" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08192), abstract. VERIFIED (1.6x, 1.25x).
- Body: "We obtain the highest speedups (∼1.6×) with Llama3-8B and Phi3-small models ... speedups ranging from 1.09× up to 1.61×" — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08192), results section. VERIFIED.
- Setup: Huawei Atlas 800T A2, 4x Kunpeng 920 CPUs, 8x Ascend 910B NPUs; each NPU has an L2 cache of 192 MB, 64 GB HBM, 1.6 TB/s off-chip bandwidth, 800 TOPS int8 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.08192), experimental setup section. Exact vs lossy: the method overlaps memory reads with communication, so it does not change the computation; numerical equivalence was not checked in this pass.
- The "80 MiB" and "Infinity Cache" terms do NOT appear in the paper. The notes said the same ("the abstract summary does not mention Infinity Cache") and the full text confirms it.

### Inferences
- The 1.6x is an end-to-end, multi-NPU number that includes communication. Our single-GPU expert-warming target cannot be sized from it.
- The paper's prefetch target is the L2 cache, which is 192 MB per NPU; a 80 MiB Infinity Cache on RDNA3 is a different structure, so the mapping needs its own measurement.

### Gaps
- Infinity Cache sizing on RDNA3 and a warm-up effect measurement are not from this paper.

The notes' statement that the intro and the notes' Section 4.4 disagree on DeepSeek-V3.1 TPOT is CONFIRMED.

### Cited Findings
- Plan A36 row: "exact", "MonoMoE 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244". The paper: "Across all evaluated distributions, model shapes, and token counts, cosine similarity remains at least 0.998" (accuracy check against FP32 reference) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04244). DISCREPANT: lossy.
- Kernel speedups (abstract and kernel results): "up to 1.54x over vLLM Triton Grouped GEMM" and "2.20-3.84x faster" than an FP8 FlashMoE adaptation; per-model Grouped GEMM speedups at B in {1,2,4,8}: 1.17-1.54x (Qwen3.5-35B), 1.02-1.20x (Qwen3.5-122B), 1.05-1.19x (GLM-5.2), 1.03-1.30x (DeepSeek-V3.1) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04244), abstract and kernel results section. VERIFIED.
- Notes' "exact or lossy" line: "lossy relative to an FP32 reference (FP8 casts)" — CONFIRMED.
- End-to-end TPOT (abstract and end-to-end results): Qwen3.5-35B 9.9-18.7% reduction at B 1-8; Qwen3.5-122B 9.1-11.5%; GLM-5.2 2.9-16.7%. DeepSeek-V3.1 (TP=8): introduction says "TPOT ranges from a 1.2% regression to a 3.7% reduction"; the end-to-end results section gives 12.8%, 8.3%, 4.7%, 3.5% reduction at B 1, 2, 4, 8 (TPOTs 8.98 / 10.00 / 10.96 / 13.90 ms vs 10.3 / 10.9 / 11.5 / 14.4 ms). The two ranges disagree, as the notes said. CONFIRMED.
- Setup: NVIDIA H200 (141 GB), vLLM with CUDA Graphs, models Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.1, 256 experts top-8 (notes; H200 confirmed in the PDF text).
- "Weight-major" = CTA tiling over expert-weight tiles with the decode token tile on the tensor-core N dimension (SwapAB), not a weight layout change: consistent with the notes (method section).

### Inferences
- The plan's A36 is about Q2_0 and a weight-major layout on RDNA3. MonoMoE's gains are FP8 and H200-specific; nothing in it transfers to Q2_0 without re-measurement.

### Gaps
- No VRAM-resident vs offloaded split is discussed in the paper (notes, confirmed by absence in the text).

## Marconi (arXiv 2411.19379), plan A11 and notes

### Takeaway
VERIFIED with attribution fixes. The "34.4x token hit rate" and "71.1% or 617 ms" headline are in the abstract, but the 71.1% and 617 ms come from two different traces (ShareGPT and SWE-Bench). The eviction-only gain over LRU is 19.0% to 219.7%. The -24.7% P95 TTFT figure is Marconi against SGLang+, not against LRU eviction. The plan's 0.5-0.8% is a repo replay estimate, not a paper number.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract: "up to 34.4× higher token hit rates (71.1% or 617 ms lower TTFT)" — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19379). VERIFIED as text.
- P95 TTFT by trace, vs vLLM+ (results section): 36.1% (275.4 ms) LMSys, 71.1% (103.3 ms) ShareGPT, 46.8% (617.0 ms) SWE-Bench; vs SGLang+: 17.2% (131.1 ms), 12.8% (18.5 ms), 24.7% (325.7 ms) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19379), latency paragraph. VERIFIED. So the notes' "71.1% is ShareGPT, 617 ms and 46.8% SWE-Bench" is CORRECT, and the abstract merges two traces.
- Eviction-only vs LRU (results section and key findings): "FLOP-aware eviction improves the token hit rate by 19.0-219.7%", with P95 wins of 219.7% (SWE-Bench), 45.6% (LMSys), 19.0% (ShareGPT) — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19379). VERIFIED. The notes' "P95 TTFT -24.7% on SWE-Bench" is the Marconi vs SGLang+ system comparison, not the eviction-only ablation. Attribution needs fixing in the notes.
- Hit-rate averages: 4.5x (LMSys), 7.3x (ShareGPT), 34.4x (SWE-Bench) against fine-grained checkpointing (results section). VERIFIED.
- Setup: main results use a 7B hybrid model with {4, 24, 28} Attention/SSM/MLP layers; p4d.24xlarge with eight A100-40GB GPUs (experimental setup). Jamba-1.5-Mini (12B active / 52B total) on four A100-40GB GPUs with vLLM is used for the TTFT insights. The notes' "7B hybrid, 4xA100" mixes the two; the 4xA100 setup is for Jamba. FP16 throughout.
- Exact or lossy: caching with exact-match reuse of states; no numerical error claim checked.

### Inferences
- The FLOP-aware eviction gain is trace-dependent: the largest win is SWE-Bench (the most agent-like trace), and ShareGPT is the smallest (19.0%).
- The notes' "+19.0% to +219.7%" is the right range for the eviction-only comparison; it should not be read as a TTFT gain.

### Gaps
- The FreeToken paper (2608.16157) in the A11 note was not opened in this batch.
- The plan's "0.5-0.8% at best" is a repo replay number, not from Marconi.
