## A39 Slipstream, arXiv 2605.08580 (Chen, Pan, Dai, Netravali)

- Plan / notes cite: "-39.7% latency (authors)" (MOD_PLAN candidate row A39; batch6 note); compaction 26-44% of query time; accuracy +2.6 to +8.8 points SWE-bench, +1.3 to +4.6 BrowseComp.
- Paper:
  - Abstract: accuracy up to +8.8 points over synchronous compaction, "reducing end-to-end latency by up to 39.7%". Section 5.2 gives a latency range of 11.3-39.7% across configurations (line ~483).
  - Compaction is "26-44%" of end-to-end latency (intro, line ~51; Section 3 / Figure, line ~285).
  - Table 1 (line ~390-402): Qwen3.5-9B SWE-bench 6k 22.0 -> 30.8 (+8.8); Seed-OSS-36B SWE-bench 6k 30.6 -> 37.4 (+6.6), 8k 38.0 -> 40.6 (+2.6); BrowseComp 48k 52.7 -> 57.3 (+4.6), 24k +1.3, 32k +3.4, 64k +2.7.
- Verdict: VERIFIED. The 39.7% is an "up to" figure against the synchronous baseline, not a typical number.
- Setup: Qwen3.5-9B on one H100 80GB; Seed-OSS-36B-Instruct on two GPUs; vLLM 0.19.1; SWE-bench Verified and BrowseComp.
- Exact or lossy: lossy. Compaction changes the context the agent sees; the paper adds a judge that validates the summary, and the accuracy gain is attributed to the judge (Section 5.3, "Async-only" keeps the latency gain with accuracy close to Sync).
- Note for the plan: the "tool windows" framing is ours. The paper's unit is the compaction running in parallel with the agent; its "next-k trajectory" overlap is 2.1 steps on BrowseComp and 3.7 on SWE-bench (Section 4.1).

## A42 Recursive Language Models, arXiv 2512.24601 (Zhang, Kraska, Khattab)

- Plan / notes cite: "+25.6% median vs compaction (authors, GPT-5)"; "+25.6% over compaction on RLM's benchmark"; against Claude Code with offloading "0 to +16.7%"; BrowseComp+ +8.7%; OOLONG-Pairs 58.0 vs 6.5.
- Paper:
  - Abstract (line ~21-22): on GPT-5, "a median across the evaluated benchmarks of 26% against compaction, 130% against CodeAct with sub-calls, and 13% against Claude Code". The 26% is rounded; the exact string 25.6 does not appear in the text.
  - Derived from Table 1 (GPT-5 block, RLM depth=1 vs Compaction agent): CodeQA 62.0 vs 58.0 (+6.9%), BrowseComp+ (1K) 91.3 vs 70.5 (+29.5%), OOLONG 56.0 vs 46.0 (+21.7%), OOLONG-Pairs 58.0 vs 0.1 (very large). The median of the four per-task relative gains is (21.7 + 29.5) / 2 = 25.6%. The plan's number is that median.
  - Table 1 Claude Code (+ context offloading), Claude Opus 4.1 row: CodeQA 62.0, BrowseComp+ 84.0, OOLONG 48.0, OOLONG-Pairs 6.5.
- Verdict: VERIFIED for the +25.6% median (it is a median of four tasks, not a single benchmark, so "on RLM's benchmark" in the notes is wrong wording). Derived, not stated in the paper; the paper states 26%.
- Caveat (DISCREPANT in framing, not in numbers): the Claude Code + offloading comparisons mix models. RLM rows are GPT-5 (with GPT-5-mini sub-calls) and the offloading row is Claude Opus 4.1. The +8.7% BrowseComp+ (91.3 vs 84.0) and the OOLONG-Pairs 58.0 vs 6.5 are therefore cross-model, not a like-for-like gain. CodeQA 62.0 vs 62.0 and OOLONG 56.0 vs 48.0 are correctly read from the table.
- Setup: GPT-5, Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B and Claude Opus 4.1 as root models; tasks CodeQA (LongBench-v2 split, 23K-4.2M tokens), BrowseComp-Plus (1K docs, 6-11M tokens), OOLONG (131K), OOLONG-Pairs (32K). Baselines: compaction agent, CodeAct, OpenCode, Claude Code.
- Exact or lossy: lossy. The metric is task quality, not speed; costs are reported separately (Table 1 cost columns; RLM is "cheaper or comparable" in the paper's words).

## A51 Quest, arXiv 2406.10774 (Tang et al., ICML 2024)

- Plan cites: "2.2-7.0x attention (paper, lossy)"; notes (batch7) quote the abstract as "up to 2.23x self-attention speedup" and "reduces inference latency by 7.03x".
- Paper:
  - Abstract (line ~33-36): "Quest can achieve up to 7.03x self-attention speedup, which reduces inference latency by 2.23x while performing well on tasks with long dependencies with negligible accuracy loss." The batch7 note swaps the two numbers.
  - Section 4.3.2 (line ~581-587): 7.03x is self-attention time vs FlashInfer at 32K, token budget 2048, Llama-2-7B. The end-to-end figure is 1.74x with FP16 weights and 2.23x with 4-bit weights, at 32K and budget 2048.
- Verdict: DISCREPANT. The 2.2 in the plan is the end-to-end number with 4-bit weights, not an attention number; 7.03x is the only attention number. Plan text should read "7.03x self-attention, 2.23x end-to-end with 4-bit weights (1.74x FP16)".
- Setup: one RTX 4090, FP16 FlashInfer baseline, Llama-2-7B, single-batch, 32K context, token budget 2048.
- Exact or lossy: lossy (top-K pages chosen from query-dependent bounds; accuracy checked on LongBench, six tasks, under a loss-less accuracy constraint).
- Plan's "a few % at 100K+" for this item is not in the paper (not checked).

## A51 Twilight, arXiv 2502.02770 (Lin et al.)

- Plan cites: decode gain (paper, lossy); notes cite "3.9x end-to-end per-token latency", "up to 98% tokens pruned".
- Paper:
  - Abstract/Conclusion (line ~30 and ~721): prunes up to 98% of tokens, "a 15.4x speedup for the self-attention operator and a 3.9x reduction in the end-to-end per-token latency"; Twilight adds 1.4x on its base algorithm.
  - Section 5.2 (line ~618-620): end-to-end Quest-Twi up to 3.9x vs FlashInfer, and 1.35x vs Quest without Twilight, batch sizes 32-256. Fig. 7 text (line ~614-616): Quest-Twi up to 15.8x vs FlashAttention2 for self-attention, FlashInfer-Twi up to 6.5x.
- Verdict: VERIFIED as stated, with the baseline: 3.9x is against FlashInfer, not against dense attention. The 15.4x self-attention figure is also in the abstract.
- Setup: single A100 GPU; LongBench for efficiency; models Longchat-7B-v1.5-32k, LLaMA2-7B-Chat, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct for accuracy.
- Exact or lossy: lossy (adaptive top-p pruning on sparse attention). Accuracy: Table 4 average +1.2% over the base (Quest) on LongBench at the stated budget.

## A50 LongSpec, arXiv 2502.17421 (Yang, Du et al., NTU 2025)

- Plan / notes cite: "up to 3.26x over strong Flash Attention baselines" and "2.25x on AIME24 with QwQ"; the batch7 note attributes 2.25x to the abstract.
- Paper:
  - Abstract (lines ~58-63): "up to a 3.26x speedup over strong Flash Attention baselines across five long-context understanding datasets, as well as a 2.34x reduction in wall-clock time on four math reasoning tasks with the QwQ model". Text (line ~103): 3.26x is against Flash Attention2; "up to a 7x speedup over common baselines using the HuggingFace implementation" (not in the notes).
  - 2.25x appears only in the table on AIME24 (tokens/s 18.92 -> 42.63, tau 3.82, line ~541); AMC is 2.33x in the same table.
- Verdict: 3.26x VERIFIED. The 2.25x is DISCREPANT in attribution: the abstract's wall-clock figure is 2.34x over four math tasks; 2.25x is AIME24 only.
- Setup: target models 7B, 8B, 13B (LLaMA-2 / LLaMA-3.1) and QwQ-32B; training on eight A100 80GB; inference float16 on a single A100 80GB.
- Exact or lossy: lossless by the paper's framing (speculative decoding: draft proposes, target verifies). Not the same mechanism as the plan's A50 (a verify-window attention kernel that reads prefix K/V once). The plan's 200K number (48.6 -> 88.9 tok/s) is a fork measurement on a 5080, not from this paper, and the paper has no 200K attention-window number. The plan's A50 "exact" claim does not come from LongSpec. Target-side verification in LongSpec uses a Triton mask-attention kernel combined with Flash Attention (Section 3, around lines 249-265, read partially).

## A31 Gated DeltaNet, arXiv 2412.06464 (Yang, Kautz, Hatamizadeh, ICLR 2025)

- Plan cites: "5-11% of cold prefill (est.)"; MOD_PLAN notes this is unsourced (batch5 note).
- Paper:
  - Only throughput figure is Figure 3: training throughput of 1.3B models on a single H100 GPU (line ~630-640): Gated DeltaNet "essentially the same throughput as DeltaNet", both "slightly slower than Mamba2 (2-3K tokens/sec)". Transformer++ is fastest at 2K context.
  - Chunk size C is kept symbolic in the chunkwise algorithm (Section 3, lines ~148-158); no numeric chunk size for experiments.
  - No prefill speedup, no inference speedup, no AMD or consumer GPU figure.
- Verdict: NOT FOUND for the 5-11% number. The paper gives no prefill gain. The plan's "unsourced" label is correct; do not cite the paper for this number.
- Setup: 1.3B models, single H100, training throughput.
- Exact or lossy: exact reformulation (chunkwise parallel form of the gated delta rule); numerics on the GPU are the only question.
