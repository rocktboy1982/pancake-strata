# The active November forks bring small ports

Of the 128 Strata forks with their own unique commits after 1 November 2026 (2,560 commits), **none brings an exact double-digit jump in decode on RX 7900 XT + 5700G**. They do bring three useful things. The first is a set of **seven tests that need only env vars or flags**, for switches we already have but have not measured: `STRATA_LOOKAHEAD=0`, `STRATA_PREFILL_COPY_WAIT_DEDUPE=1` × `STRATA_PREFILL_ROUTED_STREAM=1`, `STRATA_MMVF_ROWS=1`, `STRATA_MMVQ_R=2/4`, `--spec-min-p 0.7` and the `STRATA_PARK_ASYNC=1` check. The second is **a bundle of small, exact ports for the HIP kernels**. The most solid of them was found independently by two forks: the pointers to expert weights are read as *flat* memory instead of *global*. Disassembling our gfx1100 build confirms the problem exists for us too, including in the gate/up kernel that the fast-hip preset runs. The third is **a group of ports for agent turns**: `PF_AHEAD`, `PARK_REWIND_MAX` and `REFILL_ASYNC`, from 0xluk. Together they gave +35% in prompt reading on agent turns (measured by the author on 2x RTX 3060). The figure is for the whole bundle, not a single change, but it targets exactly the "staged" path that our turns under 4,096 tokens take. The only big lever stays a lossy one: **fewer experts per token** (K=9/8 of 10). On another model it gave +14-19% with small losses on code, but it needs a quality gate. On correctness, two bugs from the forks affect our daily preset: the prompt cache root when the system prompt is under 2,048 tokens, and an unbounded write in KV streaming with `--kv-resident`. A third bug must be fixed before the already planned `PREFILL_LAYER_MAJOR` test. About our bimodal runs, the forks bring contradictory evidence for the hugepages hypothesis and a new explanation to check: look-ahead filling the page cache for nothing.

## How we went from 4,650 forks to 128 and what we corrected on the way

The fork list came from the GitHub API: **4,546 direct forks listed** out of the ~4,650 of `Niko1221/Strata`. Of these, 955 received a push after 2026-11-01, and 631 received a push after their creation date (that is, they are not plain clones). **179 are ahead of upstream main**, 136 have commits dated after 11-01, and **128 have commits that appear in no other fork** (unique sha). These 2,560 unique commits were split into 6 batches and triaged by commit subject. Diffs were opened only for commits that touch the engine. Each finding was checked with grep in our tree (`/home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata`, HEAD `39f7a3da3`). All speed figures below are **measured by the author, on the author's hardware**. None is a pancake measurement, except those explicitly marked.

During synthesis we corrected two conclusions from the batch notes by checking them in code. First: `STRATA_POOL_DAG=1` from KhaledElzarw is not new for us. Mode 7 in `src/kernels/cpu/pool.cpp:1309` does the same thing: a single phase per layer, and a down task waits only for its own expert, quantized by the last gate/up task. The mode is active through `STRATA_POOL_FUSE_PHASES=1` in the fast-hip preset and was measured by us at +2.5% on code editing. Only `DAG=2` is new, which starts down tasks in the order gate/up finishes. Second: `STRATA_MMVF_ROWS=1` is **not** the default on HIP, as the batch 6 note said. `arch_defaults.cpp` enables it only on gfx1151 (it returns early for any other architecture), and the fast-hip preset does not set it. So it remains a valid env test on gfx1100.

## Why GPU gains will be small and where they still matter

Our rocprofv3 profile from 2026-11-09 (`bench/results/2026-11-09-7900xt-gpuprof/run_kernel_stats.csv`) sets the ceiling for everything that is a GPU kernel. **`combine_cpu_k10_vec4` takes 38.3% of GPU kernel time**, and that means the GPU waiting for the rows of the experts computed on the CPU. The VRAM expert kernels that 2.1 and 3.3 target (`expdec_gu<22>` 5.83%, `native_down_multi<42>` 5.79%) together make ~13%. Even with the 20-25% reduction reported by this-cute-world on RDNA4, the decode gain comes out at a few percent at most, and only in windows where the GPU path is the critical one. That is why the group 2 ports make sense as **a single bundle build** (2.1 + 2.2 + 2.3 + 2.4 + 2.8), tested first as a group and split only if the group shows something. Each is exact, a few dozen lines, and verifiable through the ISA. The exception is 2.1, which has a precondition already confirmed for us (`flat_load` in exactly the kernel fast-hip runs), two independent sources and a 0-9% figure on our own IQ2 formats. It is the most likely to show on its own.

On the CPU side, which is what actually limits us, the batch brings less than it seemed. Most of bsorensen110's CPU/GPU overlap stack (+4-6.5% for him) we already have: `POOL_FUSE_PHASES`, `ADAPT_OVERLAP`, GPU look-ahead. The rest (3.6, 2.5, 2.9) is realistically 1-3% in total. The test with the best chance remains the cheapest: **`STRATA_LOOKAHEAD=0`**. Our log from 2026-11-10 shows in the same startup "routing-aware prefetch on (... file tier)" and "mapped pinned cache complement ready: resident 30.50 GiB". So a thread reads the picks and does read-ahead through `warm()` into the page cache on ntfs3, for pages that decode never reads. On a 57 GB system, where the page cache competes with the complement and with the direct PLE reader, the gain can be larger than the CPU cost alone. That is an inference. Spark-NV's figure (+12.8%) comes from a 7800X3D with DDR5.

For agent turns, where opencode feels the latency, the value is in 0xluk's group. Item 78 in `MOD_PLAN.md` confirms that our preset (`STRATA_PREFILL_CPU_SHARE=0.6`, `_MAX=4096`) sends any agent delta under 4,096 tokens down the staged path. On this path, at 32K we read at 1,757 tok/s versus 2,580 on the streamed path. `PF_AHEAD` fills exactly the copy gap during attention on this path. `PARK_REWIND_MAX` and `REFILL_ASYNC` each remove 0.1-0.3 s from every turn's TTFT. On PCIe 4.0 x16 the copy is twice as cheap as for the author, so the gain from `PF_AHEAD` will probably be smaller than +35%. The gap on the copy engine stays the same, though.

## What the batch closes and what it says about the bimodal runs

Several ideas are closed by direct evidence. **Experts on the iGPU** lose: serboox moved some experts to an Iris Xe through ggml-vulkan and fell from 30.5 to 10.9 tok/s at share 0.25. Even with a single entry per layer it got 16.1 versus 31.1, because an iGPU call costs ~2 ms, as much as the whole pool on one layer, and the iGPU shares package power and DRAM bandwidth with the cores ([TUNING.md](https://github.com/serboox/strata/blob/main/docs/TUNING.md)). For the Vega in the 5700G, which also drives the desktop on the same DDR4, the case is even weaker. The same author closed **IQ prefetch distance** (0/2048/4096/8192 B: 35.28/35.24/35.15/35.23 tok/s) and **MTP depth 3** (-6.4% versus 2). This agrees with `STRATA_Q2_PREFETCH` being neutral for us. **AVX-512-only kernels** do not help us, because the 5700G has no AVX-512: Q4_K/IQ3_S/IQ4_XS from serboox, Q2_0 from bsorensen110, the Zen 5 set from Spark-NV. Only the idea of checking full groups without a masked tail in `q2_avx2_rows.inl` remains. **`STRATA_GGML_PDEP`** ([bsorensen110 `084c122106`](https://github.com/bsorensen110/Strata/commit/084c122106)) fixes a microcoded pdep on Zen 1/2. Zen 3 has fast pdep, so we have nothing to fix. Also dropped are `STRATA_POOL_UMWAIT` (Zen 3 has no WAITPKG), `FUSE_SWIGLU_GU` (clearly slower even for the author; we have `EXPDEC_SWIGLU`), `STRATA_SKIP_THR` (sterlenjohnson: no gain), `ROUTE_TAIL_SKIP=7` and masked pruning. svgop deleted the latter after MMLU dropped from 92.9% to 78.6% ([`c8286d5a27`](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/c8286d5a27)). The last one is a useful warning for 4.1: the gate must include scored tasks, not just KLD.

About **our bimodal runs (~25% of runs slower)** the evidence is contradictory. For hugepages: the `pancake-flashnext.log` log shows **38 startups with "transparent huge pages NOT requested: only 6 GiB of free 2 MiB blocks for a 33 GiB arena"**. So for us THP is often skipped because of fragmentation, and that can produce two regimes. On the other hand, serboox's microbenchmark, with an access pattern close to ours (6 threads, random 1.9 MB blocks), gives **52.4 GB/s from 4 KB pages and 53.2 GB/s from 2 MB pages**, that is, no bandwidth gain. svgop wrote on 2 November that the family that "moves bytes faster (hugepages/TLB/...)" is closed ([`30a7ef7baa`](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/30a7ef7baa)), and then still measured +4.9% on IQ2_XS with the hugetlb pool ([`df1a9c9000`](https://github.com/svgop/Strata/commit/df1a9c9000)). The honest conclusion: hugepages may explain part of the difference, but not ~25%, and they are not proven. The APU/iGPU hypothesis, that is DDR4 bandwidth and package power shared with the Vega that drives the desktop, gets indirect support from serboox's measurement. It is now being measured with the new telemetry in the harness (spec 10, marked "done" in `TEST_PLAN.md`). The batch adds a third candidate, which is an inference: **look-ahead read-ahead into the page cache** may compete variably with the complement, depending on how much page cache is free at startup. Test 1.1 checks it directly. There is also a method lesson from phoenixclyde: cold prefill TTFT has a coefficient of variation of 71-96%, so **any prompt A/B needs n ≥ 6** ([`f3a02b0296`](https://github.com/phoenixclyde/Strata/commit/f3a02b0296)).

## Conclusion

The November batch confirms and sharpens the conclusion of the previous report. The active forks work on other hardware (RDNA4, gfx906, NVIDIA, Ascend), so what carries over to gfx1100 are patterns, not ready-made kernels. Of these, the most valuable was found twice independently: the compiler emits flat loads for pointers from tables, and on RDNA this serializes LDS behind DRAM. It is worth searching for systematically in all of `iq_kernels.cu`, not only in the two named kernels. The batch also shows a useful asymmetry. The cheapest tests (1.1, 1.2/1.3) target I/O and sync waste that our logs already show, while GPU kernel gains are capped by the 38% of time in which the GPU waits on the CPU.

For the daily opencode experience, the next felt gain will probably come from agent turn TTFT (`PF_AHEAD`, `PARK_REWIND_MAX`, `REFILL_ASYNC`), not from decode tok/s. The only double-digit decode lever stays lossy: K=9/8, alongside turbo-hip. And it stays conditional on the same quality gate on scored tasks, because the batch also brings the counterexample (MMLU 92.9 → 78.6% with masked pruning). Otherwise, correctness needs a little order: the cache root and the KV guard affect the daily preset, and the layer-major scratch fix must come before an already planned test.
