# Starve the CPU, then feed the GPU

The fastest route from the current ~112 tok/s toward 150+ on one RX 7900 XT and a Ryzen 7 5700G is to cut the bytes the CPU must read for missed experts. Kernel work on the GPU comes second. **The first config win is already measured: `--pcie-frac 0` lifts the six-reply mean from 111.7 to 118.4 tok/s (+6%) and decode after an 11.7K prompt from 92.5 to 104.3 (+13%) (local measurement, round A)**, so it should become the gfx1100 default. Each decode verify window lasts ~24 ms, carries ~2.7 committed tokens, and includes ~5.5 ms where the GPU idles waiting on CPU expert rows. In each window the CPU reads ~355 MB of IQ2_XS experts at ~33 GB/s (local measurement, offline analysis). Three exact levers can roughly halve that traffic. The cheapest is faster adaptation of the VRAM tier (79.6% to 85.0% of routed entries served from VRAM in replay). Next is a reuse-aware admission policy that approaches per-layer LRU's 89.5%. The third is a bandwidth push on the AVX2 pool from ~33 toward ~40 GB/s. Together they should remove most of the wait and bring the engine to roughly 140-146 tok/s. At that point the GPU's own ~18.5 ms per window becomes the ceiling, and kernel, graph and arch-switch work (which today shows 0% because it hides under the CPU wait) starts to pay. Getting past 150 needs both halves plus more committed tokens per window. Literature claims of 1.5-2x mostly come from PCIe 4.0/5.0 rigs, data-center GPUs or lossy methods, and shrink to single-digit percentages on this box. The large lossy levers (cache-aware routing, Expert Deferral, tail-row rerouting) belong behind opt-in flags.

Round A of the local A/B runs (load-gated, quiet machine) is in and is used throughout: `STRATA_SPEC_COST=1`, `--pcie-frac 0`, DFlash2, `--spec 5/6`, draft-vocabulary variants, and the upstream "group A" arch switches. **The adaptive-tier swap-budget, kv-resident, select-long and lossy opt-in arms are still running, and their results are not in this report.**

## Where the 24 ms window goes, and why GPU tuning showed 0%

The bottleneck has a simple structure. At ~112 tok/s with windows of ~24 ms, each window commits about 2.7 tokens, which matches a 4-row MTP chain at ~75% acceptance (1 + 0.75 + 0.56 + 0.42). Inside a window, every MoE layer splits its routed experts. GPU-resident experts run on the 7900 XT, and missed experts run on the 5700G's AVX2 pool, which already decodes each IQ2_XS block once per window and applies it to all rows (local measurement). An offline replay found ~260 distinct CPU experts per 4-row window, ~355 MB, which is ~11 ms of DRAM reads at 33 GB/s (local measurement). The CPU work overlaps the GPU's expert phase of the same layer. What does not overlap shows up as the ~5.5 ms GPU wait, which leaves ~18.5 ms of GPU-side time per window (derived: 24 − 5.5).

Two consequences follow, and they drive the whole plan. First, without deferring outputs (which is lossy), the CPU can hide only behind the GPU's expert phase, about 5.3 ms per window (11 ms of CPU work minus the 5.5 ms wait). **Removing the wait exactly therefore means getting CPU work down to ~5.3 ms, i.e. roughly halving the missed-expert bytes.** Second, GPU optimisations help only where the GPU sits on the critical path. This explains both local results. Strata-rdna3's RDNA3 kernels gave a large gain (87-101 to 104-108 tok/s) because they shortened the non-expert GPU path. Strata-rdna3's author measured a GDN-layer megakernel at 0.0 ms because the time it saved fell inside the CPU wait (local measurement). A subtle trap follows: a faster GPU expert phase *shrinks* the window behind which the CPU hides, so GPU speedups beyond a point simply turn into more waiting until CPU bytes fall as well.

The `--pcie-frac 0` result confirms the analysis in the transfer notes. On this machine, a decode miss costs ~44 µs to compute on the CPU (1.437 MB / 33 GB/s) and ~107 µs to copy (1.437 MB / 13.4 GB/s). DMA copies also read the same dual-channel DDR4 the CPU is streaming from, so 33 + 13.4 GB/s of demand sits near the 51.2 GB/s theoretical peak. Every published hybrid design computes batch-1 misses on the CPU and copies only for reuse: Fiddler ([arXiv 2402.07033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07033)), KTransformers ([SOSP'25](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf)) and HybriMoE ([arXiv 2504.05897](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05897)). Turning the share off gave +6% on short replies and +13% after long prompts, with prompt read unchanged at 1,809 tok/s (local measurement). **Copies should be driven only by cache admission (expected reuse), never by load-balancing the current window.**

Benchmark noise matters at this scale. Any CPU load elsewhere costs 10-20% of decode because CPU experts are the bottleneck, so runs are gated on load average < 1.5 (local measurement). Several earlier arms were contaminated and are being re-run.

## What the field knows, five layers deep

Six lever families cover essentially all published work on consumer and offloaded MoE. Each was traced from survey through technique, mechanism and implementation down to applicability here.

**Expert caching and prediction carry the largest exact upside.** At 45% residency, which matches this card's 44.4% (10,909 experts), SeqMoE reports a 96.97% hit rate against 88.50% for LRU. It gets there with a Mamba2 forecaster over the next 8 decode steps and "probabilistic Belady" eviction, reaching 80% of fully-loaded decode speed on RTX 4090/5090 ([SeqMoE, arXiv 2609.12978](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12978)). Its models have 128-256 experts with top-4 to top-8, and shared experts lower local routing consistency ([arXiv 2505.16056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16056)), so a 512-expert top-10 model should see less headroom. The engine's own replay sets the real bounds: 79.6% for the adaptive tier as configured, 85.0% for one adaptation round per window with 160 swaps, 89.5% for per-layer LRU that admits on every miss, and 94.7% for the per-layer Belady optimum (local measurement). Mainline llama.cpp's new `--moe-cache-mib` cache is plain LRU that uploads misses. It reached 89% hit and 2.2x on an RTX 5090 with PCIe 4.0 on this same model family ([llama.cpp PR #29887](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29887)), and ik_llama.cpp's port reached +23.6% on a 3090 ([PR 2644](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2644)). Its upload-on-miss design is the right choice for PCIe 4.0/5.0 and the wrong one here. The vLLM RFC proposes LFRU (frequency/age) eviction that keeps "hub" experts through bursts ([vLLM #38256](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/38256)). Routing is "globally balanced, locally imbalanced", with skew growing from mid-depth ([MoE-Sieve, arXiv 2603.24044](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24044)), which argues for per-layer and per-domain treatment.

**CPU expert kernels are near the DRAM wall, so remaining gains are about bandwidth, not math.** The research notes' top kernel item was a multi-row "decode once" IQ2_XS kernel. Mainline ggml's AVX2 path is `nrc == 1` ([ggml quants.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/ggml/src/ggml-cpu/arch/x86/quants.c)), but pancake already does decode-once with software prefetch (local measurement), so that item is closed. What is left is the gap between ~33 GB/s and an achievable read rate somewhere between the 51.2 GB/s theoretical peak and an unsourced mid-40s figure. The likely causes are prefetcher ramp-up at each scattered 1.37 MiB expert and idle tails at barriers. KTransformers fuses Gate/Up/Down across all of a layer's experts into "two fused batches" and uses a dynamic sub-task queue to cut synchronization ([KTransformers SOSP'25](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf)). ik_llama.cpp's row-interleaved `_R4` layouts amortize activation loads ([ik_llama.cpp discussion #164](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/164)). LUT kernels such as T-MAC report 4-5x mpGEMV at 2 bits on AVX2 ([T-MAC, arXiv 2407.00088](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00088v2)), but they need a uniform-grid format, not IQ2_XS's E8 codebook. They would change the model's outputs and hit the same DRAM ceiling. Local thread tests agree that the pool is bandwidth-bound: 12 workers was worse (mean 99 vs 112, prefill 1,229) and nice +5 cost 4 tok/s (local measurement).

**CPU/GPU overlap is where the published big numbers live, and most of it is lossy.** KTransformers' Expert Deferral adds some routed experts' outputs one layer late, at k+2 instead of k+1, so CPU expert work overlaps the GPU's next attention. That gave +33% decode on DeepSeek-V3, up to 1.45x, with an average accuracy drop of at most 0.5% ([LMSYS blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-10-22-KTransformers)). The engine already has a verify-only form, `STRATA_VERIFY_DEFER_CPU` (local PANCAKE_FINDINGS.md). HybriMoE's dynamic intra-layer CPU/GPU split is exact and reports 1.70x over kTransformers ([arXiv 2504.05897](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05897)). On PCIe 3.0 its upload half is weak, and the `--pcie-frac 0` result shows that moving work onto that link is currently a loss. KTransformers also captures the whole hybrid forward, including host-function CPU tasks, in one CUDA graph, for up to 1.23x ([SOSP'25](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf)). That fits engines with per-layer host syncs, and pancake's verify window is already a HIP graph.

**Speculation for MoE is now cost-aware, and the engine already has the core of it.** EVICT's rule, k* = argmax E[accepted]/C(k), with C(k) profiled offline, gives about 1.15-1.25x over EAGLE-3 on A100 ([EVICT, arXiv 2605.00342](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00342v2)). That rule is structurally `STRATA_SPEC_COST`, which locally gave 112.0 vs 111.7 on short replies and **98.3 vs 92.5 (+6%) after a long prompt** (local measurement). The newer step makes cost depend on the window's predicted expert expansion. DraftExpert stops drafting when confidence q_i < τ and the marginal expert expansion exceeds a budget. Its offload ablation cuts the verify-cost multiple from 2.35x to 1.48x against fixed K=4 on an RTX 4090 with host-RAM experts ([DraftExpert, arXiv 2607.24434](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)). EcoSpec scores tree nodes by probability divided by new experts ([arXiv 2607.12696](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12696v1)), and "Limits of Speculation" fits verify cost as α·(unique experts) + β with R² 0.97-0.999 ([arXiv 2609.22156](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22156v1)). AcceptMoE reroutes low-commitment rows away from non-resident experts. That is lossy, giving 2.06x over standard speculative decoding under offload at −0.27 pp mean accuracy ([arXiv 2608.02989](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02989)). No published method exactly *truncates* tail rows mid-window based on realized misses, so that idea appears novel. External drafters disappoint here. DFlash 2 gave +39% on code over no drafter in ik_llama.cpp at ~35 tok/s ([PR 2761](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2761)). Locally, replacing MTP with DFlash2 gave **+4% on short replies but −9% after a long prompt (84.3 vs 92.5)**. Longer windows did not help either: `--spec 5` gave 111.0 and `--spec 6` gave 109.2 (local measurement). Both results are consistent with expert-union growth per extra row.

**GPU kernels on RDNA3 matter once the CPU wait is gone.** At 1-8 verify rows the projections and GPU experts are memory-bound GEMVs. WMMA's 16x16 tiles waste at least half their width, so multi-row MMVQ with dot4 integer instructions is the right shape, and llama.cpp's RDNA3 MMQ tuning found up to ~1.67x at microbatch 16 for Q2_K_S ([PR 26284](https://github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp/pull/26284)). Megakernels give 1.3-2.5x on small dense models where launch bubbles dominate ([Hazy "No Bubbles"](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles); [Mirage MPK, arXiv 2512.22219](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22219v1)). On the 5090, idle blocks prefetching the next weights into L2 was the largest single win ([Alpindale](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://blog.alpindale.net/posts/5090_decode_optimization)). For Gated DeltaNet, FlashInfer's fused T>1 verify kernel and ReplaySSM commit set the pattern ([FlashInfer gdn_decode](https://docs.flashinfer.ai/api/gdn_decode.html)). ROCm 7.2.x added AQL batch submission for graphs ([ROCm 7.2.0 notes](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/docs-7.2.0/about/release-notes.html)). On this card, ~1,000 graph nodes at ~2.4 µs each put the gap ceiling around 2.4 ms per window. Arch switches are a warning: turning on all the upstream "group A" switches together (SIDE_MIXER=2, Q8_PACKED, LFUSE, GDN_SPLIT, EXPERT_V2, TSUM, ATTN_LANECELL, MMVF_ROWS, PLE_BATCH and others) dropped the mean to **101.2 and after-long decode to 80.3** (local measurement). At least one switch hurts on gfx1100, so they must be bisected one by one.

**Prefill is PCIe-bound and already competitive.** All experts total ~35.3 GB. With ~15.7 GB resident, a full streaming pass moves ~19.6 GB at 13.4 GB/s, about 1.5 s. Throughput is therefore roughly chunk tokens divided by stream time until compute takes over, and the only real lever is tokens per pass ([SLP, arXiv 2606.10493](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10493); [MoE-Gen, arXiv 2503.09716](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09716v1)). The best published consumer prefill is ~1,200 tok/s with one RTX 5090 on PCIe 5.0 ([arXiv 2606.10493](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10493)). Pancake's 1,812 tok/s and Avalon's 1,771-1,805 on this box are in the same regime (local measurement). Strata-rdna3 reports 3,500+ tok/s on one 7900 XTX (local PANCAKE_FINDINGS.md). Lossless compression cannot raise the link rate: ZipNN found GGUF checkpoints essentially incompressible ([survey, arXiv 2608.25188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25188)).

## From 112 toward 150+: the ceiling each step hits

At ~2.7 committed tokens per window, 150 tok/s needs an ~18 ms window. The staircase below is this report's projection from the window model. It assumes each step's gains mostly stack, and that assumption must be checked against the profile split (#8).

| Step | What changes | Window | tok/s | Ceiling hit next |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline (round A) | ~24 ms (18.5 GPU + 5.5 wait) | 111.7 | CPU DRAM bytes |
| 1 | `--pcie-frac 0` + SPEC_COST (#1, #2) | ~22.7 ms | **118.4 measured** (after long 104.3) | CPU DRAM bytes; DMA contention gone |
| 2 | Faster adaptation, 85% from VRAM (#3) | ~21-21.5 ms | ~125-128 | CPU bytes; PCIe ~40% busy with swaps |
| 3 | Reuse-aware admission ~88-90% + pool at ~40 GB/s (#9, #12) | ~18.5-19 ms | ~140-146 | **GPU time** (the wait is close to zero) |
| 4 | GPU path −10-15% (#4 winners, #13), plus tail truncation and miss-aware windows (#10, #11) | ~16.5-17 ms | ~155-165 | CPU again: the shorter GPU expert phase hides less, so the hit rate needs ~92%+ (forecaster #15), bounded by Belady 94.7% |
| 5 | More committed tokens per window (better drafts on code; suffix drafts on edits) | — | 165-180 on edit-heavy replies | Expert-union growth per extra row; the PCIe admission budget |

Three things stand out. First, **CPU DRAM bandwidth is the binding ceiling through step 3.** At 33 GB/s and ~5.3 ms of overlap, the CPU can afford ~175 MB per window. That is about half of today's 355 MB, and it is what a ~90% hit rate delivers. Faster memory raises the allowance roughly in proportion to MT/s. Second, **PCIe 3.0 is a budget, not a ceiling.** Admitting ~130 experts per window would take ~14 ms of a 24 ms window (58% of the link), so admission must be selective and scheduled into CPU-idle phases. Even the Belady optimum is limited by how many swaps fit. Third, **GPU compute and launch overhead become the ceiling only after step 3**, which is why GPU kernel work should wait for the profile and not lead. Avalon already reaches 168-181 tok/s on code edits on this box with its own model (local measurement). That shows edit-heavy, high-acceptance workloads can clear 150 here. On new text, 150+ exactly requires steps 3 and 4 together. The lossy shortcuts are cache-aware routing (+25%, measured) and Expert Deferral. Stacked on step 2 they reach ~150 sooner, at a cost to exactness.

## Prefill and decode after long prompts

Prefill at ~1,810 tok/s is bound by expert streaming over PCIe 3.0 and was unchanged by `--pcie-frac 0` (1,809). The lever is tokens per streaming pass (#14). Every GB kept resident saves ~75 ms per pass, so keeping the decode tier resident through prefill helps both phases. Strata-rdna3's 3,500+ tok/s on a 7900 XTX (local PANCAKE_FINDINGS.md) suggests large headroom, though that card has 4 GB more VRAM, and its PCIe generation is not recorded in the notes. Decode after a long prompt is where the round A arms moved most: 92.5 to 104.3 with the PCIe share off, and to 98.3 with SPEC_COST. That points to DRAM contention and window sizing, not attention math, as the main cause. The remaining gap (104 vs 118) is likely tier pollution from prefill routing (#6) or QSA selection cost that scales with context ([Quest](https://huggingface.co/papers/2406.10774); [LServe, arXiv 2502.14866](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14866v2)). The pending kv-resident and select-long arms will separate those two.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. This engine is not short on clever GPU kernels or drafting tricks. It is short on DDR4 bytes, and every exact gain on new text has to be bought by reading fewer expert bytes on the CPU or reading them faster. Round A already confirmed this cheaply: the largest single win was *removing* work from the PCIe link, and the arms that added work to the CPU (longer windows, DFlash2 after long prompts, wider draft vocabularies) lost. The literature's 1.5-2x headlines come from different bottlenecks. Here the honest exact ceiling is ~140-146 tok/s from cache and bandwidth work alone, and ~155-165 once the GPU path and window policy are tightened. Past that, the Belady limit and the PCIe swap budget leave only lossy routes or better drafts.

The single most valuable next artifact is not a kernel but the offline replay extended into a simulator. It should model admission with scheduled DMA, tail truncation and miss-aware windows together against logged traces, so that #9-#11 are tuned before they are built.
