# MIT / Harvard papers on LLM decoding, speculative decoding, KV-cache, test-time compute and serving (Nov 2022 - Nov 2026), judged for pancake / Avalon

Scope notes for the report writer:
- Target setup: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (~125B MoE hybrid, Gated DeltaNet + attention, MTP head), one RX 7900 XT 20 GB, experts tiered VRAM/RAM/NVMe, single user, one slot, long growing opencode prompts. pancake = Strata fork (MTP + n-gram/prompt-lookup drafting, parked conversation cache with message-boundary checkpoints, thinking budget, adaptive expert tier). Avalon = llama.cpp fork (ngram-simple + draft-mtp).
- "Lead" = first and/or senior author at MIT/Harvard. Affiliations come from author names I know to be MIT/Harvard plus the paper's lab GitHub org (for example mit-han-lab, clinicalml). arXiv abstract pages do not show affiliations, so affiliations marked "inferred" were not read from the PDF.
- Metadata (authors, dates, venues, numbers) for most entries was checked against the arXiv API in this session ([batch query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2402.05109,2502.11517,2410.04707,2403.03870,2511.16665,2502.14866,2410.10819,2406.10774,2309.17453,2512.24601)).
- Nothing below overlaps the Stanford report (SGLang, FlexGen, H2O, Staged SD, SSD, s1, Monkeys and the rest). Excluded by scope: attention kernels and architectures (XAttention, Gated DeltaNet itself), quantization (QServe, AWQ), MoE offload, agent and NLP papers. Recursive Language Models (MIT) is listed only as an out-of-scope pointer.
- Bottom line: MIT and Harvard have few papers in this area that fit a single GPU and a single user. The most portable ones are MIT's **reasoning early-exit** work (Thought Calibration, OS-Pruner, Learning How Hard to Think). On the speculative side, MIT's Hydra is essentially what the MTP head already does. I found **no MIT or Harvard paper on speculative decoding with recurrent-state rollback for hybrid models.**

## Speculative / parallel decoding: what beats MTP + n-gram for code editing on one GPU with offloaded experts?

### Takeaway
None of the MIT speculative-decoding papers beats MTP plus n-gram for our setup. Hydra (MIT lead) shows that draft heads which see earlier draft tokens beat independent Medusa heads. Our MTP head is already that kind of sequential drafter, so the only increment left is a small top-k draft tree on the first 1-2 positions (est. 0-10%). PASTA (MIT lead, ICML 2025) and Co-LLM (MIT lead) both need fine-tuning the 125B target, so neither is feasible here. TLT (Han Lab) adds one portable idea: an online-adaptive drafter and per-step selection of the speculation strategy. The state of the art on hybrid-model rollback (ReplaySSM, STree, SpecLA, TreeWY) comes from groups other than MIT and Harvard.

### Cited Findings
- **Hydra: Sequentially-Dependent Draft Heads for Medusa Decoding.** MIT lead: Zachary Ankner, Rishab Parthasarathy, Aniruddha Nrusimha, Christopher Rinard, Jonathan Ragan-Kelley, William Brandon (MIT CSAIL; affiliation inferred). arXiv, 7 Feb 2024, no venue listed. Each draft head conditions on the earlier speculated tokens, unlike Medusa's independent heads. Hydra++ reaches up to 1.31x the throughput of Medusa and 2.70x that of autoregressive decoding. Code: github.com/zankner/Hydra. — [arXiv 2402.05109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05109)
  - Verdict: **already covered.** Qwen's MTP head conditions on the previous token and hidden state, which is the Hydra principle. The one transferable piece is Hydra's tree decoding: verify top-2 MTP candidates at positions 1-2 in a single batched verify. On a 7900 XT with offloaded experts, every extra verified token can pull in more expert weights, so a wider tree costs more here than on dense GPU models. Expected gain 0-10%, to be measured.
- **Learning to Keep a Promise / PASTA (learned asynchronous decoding).** MIT lead: Tian Jin, Ellie Y. Cheng, Zack Ankner, Nikunj Saunshi, Blake M. Elias, Amir Yazdanbakhsh, Jonathan Ragan-Kelley, Suvinay Subramanian, Michael Carbin (MIT with Google co-authors; inferred). ICML 2025, PMLR 267:27941-27956. The model is trained to emit annotations that mark semantically independent chunks, which are then decoded in parallel. Geometric-mean speedups are 1.21x-1.93x, with quality changes of +2.2% to -7.1% in length-controlled win rate. No code in the abstract. — [arXiv 2502.11517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11517)
  - Verdict: **not applicable.** It needs fine-tuning the target model and batch-parallel decoding of branches. Our single slot with expert offload gets little from extra parallel streams, and code edits are mostly sequential.
- **Learning to Decode Collaboratively with Multiple Language Models (Co-LLM).** MIT lead: Shannon Zejiang Shen, Hunter Lang, Bailin Wang, Yoon Kim, David Sontag (MIT CSAIL clinicalml; inferred). arXiv, 6 Mar 2024. A latent per-token variable learns when a base model defers to an assistant model. The joint system beats either model alone on instruction-following, domain QA and reasoning. The abstract gives no speed numbers. Code: github.com/clinicalml/co-llm. — [arXiv 2403.03870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03870)
  - Verdict: **no.** It is a quality technique, not a speed technique, and it needs training a deferral head.
- **Taming the Long-Tail: Efficient Reasoning RL Training with Adaptive Drafter (TLT).** MIT Han Lab lead: Qinghao Hu, Shang Yang, Junxian Guo, Xiaozhe Yao, Yujun Lin, Yuxian Gu, Han Cai, Chuang Gan, Ana Klimovic (ETH), Song Han. arXiv, 20 Nov 2025. Speculative decoding is used inside RL rollouts with a drafter that is trained continuously on idle GPUs, plus an adaptive rollout engine that picks the SD strategy. Over 1.7x end-to-end RL training speedup. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/fastrl. — [arXiv 2511.16665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16665)
  - Verdict: **idea only, low-moderate.** The training side is irrelevant. The transferable idea is choosing the drafting configuration online from measured acceptance. For pancake that means choosing per step between MTP, n-gram, both, or none, and the draft length, using a running acceptance estimate per context type (code edit vs prose vs tool JSON). Estimated 3-10% on mixed agent turns, mostly by not drafting where acceptance is low. Inference only.
- Hybrid-model rollback work found in this search is all **non-MIT/Harvard**. These are useful pointers but not in the MIT/Harvard list:
  - ReplaySSM (NVIDIA plus Princeton's Dao AI Lab): caches draft inputs instead of state snapshots and commits the accepted prefix by moving a pointer. NVIDIA reports 1.85x-2.10x speculative throughput on 120B-class models. — [Tri Dao blog](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/replayssm/), [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/replayssm-rethinking-state-storage-for-faster-hybrid-model-decoding/)
  - STree: tree speculative decoding for hybrid SSMs. — [arXiv 2505.14969](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14969)
  - SpecLA: linear-attention SD that recovers state from compact verification artifacts. — [arXiv 2607.16673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16673)
  - TreeWY: tree reconstruction for Gated DeltaNet hybrids. — [Modelwire summary](https://themodelwire.com/article/treewy-enables-speculative-decoding-for-linear-attention-hybrid-models-01M0RR61FJH7MW7VPVWP1AA6ZJ)
- Graft ("Draft Less, Retrieve More"): prunes the draft tree and fills the freed slots with retrieved tokens, which is close to our MTP+n-gram mix. Up to 5.41x on short context and +21.8% over EAGLE-3 on Qwen3-235B. Affiliation **not shown**, and I did not confirm it as MIT/Harvard. — [arXiv 2605.20104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20104)

### Inferences
- What beats plain MTP + n-gram on our box is engineering, not an MIT paper:
  - (a) Cheap GDN state rollback in the ReplaySSM style (non-MIT), so that rejected drafts cost no snapshot copies.
  - (b) A Graft- or Hydra-style merged tree: n-gram continuation on one branch, MTP top-1/top-2 on another.
  - (c) TLT-style online selection of the draft strategy.
- Expert offload changes the economics. Verifying k tokens in one forward pass activates the union of their experts, so more tokens per verify can mean more RAM/NVMe expert traffic. Wide trees that pay off on dense H100 setups may lose here, which favors narrow, high-acceptance drafts such as n-gram on code edits.

### Gaps
- I found no MIT or Harvard paper on MoE-aware speculation, self-speculation with layer skipping, lookahead/Jacobi decoding, or recurrent-state rollback. Lookahead and Jacobi Forcing are from UCSD/LMSYS (Hao AI Lab), not MIT. Searches with Harvard as the affiliation returned no speculative-decoding paper ([search results example](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13157v1)).
- Affiliations were not read from the PDFs. Hydra, PASTA and Co-LLM are attributed to MIT from known author affiliations and repos.

## KV-cache / prompt-cache work from MIT (Han Lab and others) relevant to one long-context user

### Takeaway
All of the Han Lab KV work (StreamingLLM, Quest, DuoAttention, LServe) cuts the cost of attention over long contexts. On our hybrid model only a minority of layers are full attention and the KV is already small, so these methods address the smaller part of decode time. They also risk dropping exact code tokens (StreamingLLM, the DuoAttention streaming heads). Quest-style query-aware page selection is the only lossless-ish, training-free option, and it pays off only at 100k+ tokens. None of these papers addresses prefix and prompt-cache reuse for one user, which is our real cost (re-prefill). The Stanford RadixAttention recommendation stays the main lever.

### Cited Findings
- **Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks (StreamingLLM).** MIT lead: Guangxuan Xiao, Yuandong Tian (Meta), Beidi Chen (CMU/Meta), Song Han, Mike Lewis (Meta). ICLR 2024, arXiv 29 Sep 2023. Keeping the first few "sink" tokens plus a recent window lets models run stably up to 4M tokens. Up to 22.2x faster than sliding-window recomputation. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/streaming-llm. — [arXiv 2309.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)
  - Verdict: **no for coding.** It evicts the middle of the context, which is where file contents and tool outputs live. The only relevant takeaway: if pancake ever does context shifting or truncation, keep the first tokens (system prompt) intact. That is already the case with the parked cache.
- **Quest: Query-Aware Sparsity for Efficient Long-Context LLM Inference.** MIT Han Lab lead: Jiaming Tang, Yilong Zhao, Kan Zhu, Guangxuan Xiao, Baris Kasikci (UW), Song Han. ICML 2024. It keeps per-page min/max key metadata and loads only the top-K KV pages relevant to the current query. Up to 2.23x self-attention speedup and up to 7.03x lower inference latency, with negligible accuracy loss. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/Quest. — [arXiv 2406.10774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10774)
  - Verdict: **low-moderate, long contexts only.** It is training-free and keeps the full KV, so it can be reverted per step. On our hybrid only the attention layers benefit. At ~100k+ tokens attention may be a noticeable share of the ~8 ms/token decode, but I measured no profile. Estimated 0-10% decode gain at very long prompts, ~0 at short ones. It needs a HIP kernel, which borders on attention-kernel work (another researcher's scope).
- **DuoAttention: Efficient Long-Context LLM Inference with Retrieval and Streaming Heads.** MIT Han Lab lead: Guangxuan Xiao, Jiaming Tang, Jingwei Zuo, Junxian Guo, Shang Yang, Haotian Tang, Yao Fu, Song Han. arXiv, 14 Oct 2024 (ICLR 2025 per lab, not in arXiv comment). Optimized gates classify heads into retrieval heads (full KV) and streaming heads (sink plus recent only). Memory falls up to 2.55x (MHA) and 1.67x (GQA). Decode speeds up 2.18x (MHA) and 1.50x (GQA), prefill 1.73x and 1.63x. With quantization, Llama-3-8B decodes at 3.3M context on one A100. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/duo-attention. — [arXiv 2410.10819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10819)
  - Verdict: **no.** The gates need gradient optimization through the model, which is impractical for a 125B MoE with offloaded experts. The memory win is irrelevant because the hybrid's KV is small, and streaming heads lose distant code.
- **LServe: Efficient Long-sequence LLM Serving with Unified Sparse Attention.** MIT Han Lab lead: Shang Yang, Junxian Guo, Haotian Tang, Qinghao Hu, Guangxuan Xiao, Jiaming Tang, Yujun Lin, Zhijian Liu, Yao Lu, Song Han. MLSys 2025. It combines static sparsity (Duo-style streaming heads) with dynamic sparsity (hierarchical, Quest-like page selection reused across decode steps). Prefill is up to 2.9x faster and decode 1.3-2.1x faster than vLLM. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/omniserve. — [arXiv 2502.14866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14866)
  - Verdict: **idea only.** The one portable trick is reusing the selected page set across several consecutive decode steps, which amortizes selection cost. It applies only if Quest-style selection is adopted. It is a serving-system paper for multi-GPU throughput, not a single-user engine.

### Inferences
- For one long-context user on a hybrid model, the cost that matters is **prefill after a cache miss**: agent and sub-agent switches, and compaction rewrites. Han Lab's sparse-attention work speeds up prefill only in the attention layers, while GDN layers and MoE experts dominate. The Stanford RadixAttention multi-entry LRU recommendation stays the priority. The MIT KV papers don't change it.
- Quest is the only MIT KV paper worth a later experiment. Its precondition is a profile showing that attention-layer decode time at 100k+ is above ~15% of the token time.

### Gaps
- I found no MIT or Harvard paper on prefix/prompt-cache reuse, KV offload to RAM/NVMe for a single user, or cache persistence. Harvard KV-cache searches returned only non-Harvard papers ([search results example: KVComp, Temple](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00579)).
- The Han Lab project listing I fetched showed only the first page (2026 items: Quant VideoGen, Lightning Weave, SMEPO). Older and other 2025-2026 inference projects on later pages were not enumerated ([Han Lab projects](https://hanlab.mit.edu/projects)).

## Test-time compute / reasoning-length efficiency from MIT/Harvard

### Takeaway
This is the most useful MIT cluster for pancake, where a thinking budget already exists. Thought Calibration (MIT, Jaakkola) uses cheap linear probes on hidden states to decide when to stop thinking, with up to 60% fewer thinking tokens in-distribution and up to 20% out-of-distribution. OS-Pruner (MIT Sloan/ORC, inferred) frames stopping as optimal stopping and cuts length 20-60%. Learning How Hard to Think (MIT, Andreas) predicts per-input difficulty to allocate compute, with up to 50% less compute at equal quality. All three can work at inference time with small trained heads and no change to the 125B weights. They map onto replacing pancake's fixed thinking budget with a learned stop signal.

### Cited Findings
- **Thought calibration: Efficient and confident test-time scaling.** MIT lead (inferred): Menghua Wu, Cai Zhou, Stephen Bates, Tommi Jaakkola. arXiv, 23 May 2025, no venue listed. The growing thought is modeled as nested reasoning trees, and thinking stops when new reasoning plateaus. Lightweight probes on hidden representations make the decision, using both reasoning structure and answer consistency. With 3 reasoning LMs and 4 datasets, thinking tokens fall up to 60% in-distribution and up to 20% out-of-distribution while performance is preserved. No code link on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.18404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18404)
  - Verdict: **highest applicability in this whole list.** pancake's C++ side has the hidden states. The steps:
    1. Log hidden states at fixed intervals (for example every 64 thinking tokens) on a few hundred recorded opencode turns.
    2. Label each point by whether the final answer or tool call would already have been the same if `</think>` had been forced there.
    3. Train a linear probe offline.
    4. At runtime, inject `</think>` when the probe is confident.
    The probe is a dot product per check, so overhead is negligible. Expected gain: 20-60% fewer thinking tokens on routine agent turns. At ~126 t/s, a 1,500-token think shortened by a third saves ~4 s per turn. The OOD figure (≤20%) is the conservative bound, since opencode traces are OOD relative to the paper's math/QA data.
- **OS-Pruner: Pruning Chains-of-Thought of Reasoning Models via Optimal Stopping.** Mohammed Ehab, Aymane El Gadarri, Vivek F. Farias, Adam Jozefiak, Ciamac C. Moallemi. Farias is MIT Sloan and Moallemi is Columbia; the arXiv page does not show affiliations, so the MIT lead is inferred. arXiv, 13 Jul 2026. A lightweight plug-in learns whether continuing to reason is worth the token cost, with a user-tunable accuracy-vs-length utility. Generation length falls 20-60% with minimal accuracy loss. No code link. — [arXiv 2607.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11089), [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11089)
  - Verdict: **high (same slot as Thought Calibration).** Its tunable utility maps naturally onto opencode effort levels: low effort means a higher token cost in the utility. I could not confirm whether it uses hidden-state features or only text features.
- **Learning How Hard to Think: Input-Adaptive Allocation of LM Computation.** MIT lead (inferred): Mehul Damani, Idan Shenfeld, Andi Peng, Andreea Bobu, Jacob Andreas. arXiv, 7 Oct 2024 (ICLR 2025 per my knowledge, not in the arXiv comment). It predicts the marginal benefit of extra compute per input and allocates decoding compute (for example best-of-k, or routing to a larger model) accordingly. Compute falls up to 50% with no quality cost, or quality rises up to 10% at a fixed budget. No code in the abstract. — [arXiv 2410.04707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04707)
  - Verdict: **moderate.** For one slot the useful form is a **pre-generation** difficulty predictor that sets the thinking budget per turn (for example tool-result echo means low, a failed test means high), using a probe on the prompt's last hidden state. It complements Thought Calibration, which decides during generation. The best-of-k part does not apply to one slot.
- **Know What You Don't Know: Uncertainty Calibration of Process Reward Models / instance-adaptive scaling.** MIT lead: Young-Jin Park, Kristjan Greenewald (MIT-IBM), Kaveh Alim, Hao Wang, Navid Azizan (MIT LIDS/MechE). NeurIPS 2025. A calibrated PRM drives how many candidates or steps to explore, matching accuracy with "as little as one-half the computation." — [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/smarter-way-large-language-models-think-about-hard-problems-1204), [arXiv 2506.09338](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09338)
  - Verdict: **no.** It needs a PRM plus multi-candidate search, which does not fit single-slot decode at 125B.
- **Critical Thinking: Which Kinds of Complexity Govern Optimal Reasoning Length?** Harvard co-author: Celine Lee, Alexander Rush (Cornell, lead), Keyon Vafa (Harvard). IJCNLP-AACL 2025. The optimal reasoning length depends on task structure (DFA properties), and restricting answers to the "critical length" improves accuracy. — [arXiv 2504.01935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01935v1.pdf), [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-ijcnlp-aacl/2025.ijcnlp-long.57.pdf)
  - Verdict: **conceptual support only** for per-task-type budgets.
- MIT CSAIL "Plan and Budget" (with Virginia Tech, UVA, MSU): splits a task into sub-questions with token budgets. Primary paper verified: **arXiv 2505.16122**, "Plan and Budget: Effective and Efficient Test-Time Scaling on Reasoning Large Language Models", Junhong Lin, Julian Shun (MIT CSAIL) + Xinyue Zeng, Jie Zhu, Dawei Zhou (Virginia Tech), Song Wang (UVA), Jun Wu (MSU); ICLR 2026. The paper's own claims match the secondary source: up to 70% accuracy gains, 39% token reduction, and +193.8% on its E3 efficiency metric, training-free at test time. — [arXiv 2505.16122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16122), [code](https://github.com/junhongmit/P-and-B)
- Han Lab "Lightning Weave" (arXiv 2026) distills accuracy and efficiency capabilities into one student, using ~10% fewer response tokens on some benchmarks. It needs training, so not applicable. — [Han Lab projects](https://hanlab.mit.edu/projects)

### Inferences
- Concrete pancake plan: replace or augment the fixed thinking budget with (1) a per-turn budget from a prompt-end probe (Learning How Hard to Think) and (2) an in-flight stop probe (Thought Calibration / OS-Pruner). Both are linear heads on hidden states, trained offline from logged opencode turns. Labeling can be done by forcing `</think>` at checkpoints during replay and comparing the resulting tool call. This needs extra offline decode time but no backprop through the 125B model.
- Expected impact is larger than any speculative-decoding tweak in this list. Thinking tokens are a large share of agent wall time, and a 20-40% cut maps directly to latency. Validate on the Terminal-Bench subset proposed in the Stanford report.

### Gaps
- None of these papers evaluates agentic coding turns or tool-call correctness. All are math and QA. The transfer to opencode is unmeasured.
- I found no MIT or Harvard paper specifically on early exit of reasoning for hybrid recurrent models, or on interaction with MTP drafting. Stopping thinking early also changes draft acceptance, which is untested.
- Code availability for Thought Calibration and OS-Pruner: none found on the abstract pages.

## Serving systems with MIT/Harvard involvement suitable for a single GPU, single user

### Takeaway
MIT's serving-system output (OmniServe/LServe, and QServe, which is excluded as quantization) targets multi-GPU, high-throughput batched serving on NVIDIA. None of it is a fit as an engine for a single-user AMD box. The useful content is the sparse-attention ideas discussed above. I found no Harvard-led LLM serving system in this window.

### Cited Findings
- LServe / OmniServe (MIT Han Lab, MLSys 2025): unified sparse attention serving, 2.9x prefill and 1.3-2.1x decode vs vLLM. CUDA-centric. Code: github.com/mit-han-lab/omniserve. — [arXiv 2502.14866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14866)
- StreamingLLM ships as a recipe integrated into other engines rather than a standalone server. — [arXiv 2309.17453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)
- Out-of-scope pointer: **Recursive Language Models** (MIT lead: Alex L. Zhang, Tim Kraska, Omar Khattab; arXiv 31 Dec 2025). The LM processes inputs up to 100x its context window by recursively calling itself over a REPL-held context. Median gains on GPT-5 are +26% vs compaction and +13% vs Claude Code at comparable cost. Code: github.com/alexzhang13/rlm. It is an agent/scaffold paper (other researcher's scope), but it bears on compaction strategy for long opencode sessions. — [arXiv 2512.24601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601)

### Inferences
- For serving, pancake and Avalon should keep borrowing from Stanford (RadixAttention-style multi-entry cache) and non-academic engines. MIT contributes no single-GPU serving system to adopt.

### Gaps
- Harvard systems groups (for example Minlan Yu, H. T. Kung, Brooks/Wei) may have LLM-serving papers that searches did not surface. Harvard-affiliation searches for speculative decoding, KV cache and reasoning efficiency returned only non-Harvard work, apart from the Vafa co-authorship ([search example](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05202v2)).
- Tool-call budget limited the enumeration of MIT 2026 papers. A direct scan of MIT CSAIL and Harvard SEAS 2026 arXiv listings could find more.
