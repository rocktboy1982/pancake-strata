# The Qwen report confirms pancake and corrects Avalon

The most useful Asian paper for the project is the technical report of the daily model itself, **"On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture" (Qwen Team, lead, arXiv 2608.30320)**, read together with the SGLang launch blog. I checked them against the pancake source and the verdict is surprisingly good: **pancake already does almost everything Qwen recommends for MTP**. The daily Q2_0 preset proposes 4 tokens per verification (`--spec 4`), exactly the depth at which Qwen reports **4.07 accepted tokens per verification**. Window extension depends on draft confidence (`--spec-min-p 0.5`), the MTP layer does not run the n-gram table (PLE) and has no QSA indexer, and the n-gram rows of the draft tokens are read ahead. **Avalon is the one below the recommendation**, with `--spec-draft-n-max 2`. For it, the 3/4 A/B is the cheapest action in this report. What we do not exploit yet comes from three other sources: the expert deferral from **KTransformers (Tsinghua MADSys, lead, SOSP '25)**, which needs a rank rule next to the existing weight threshold in item 8; **per-expert barriers in the CPU pool (Tsinghua, OSDI '26)**, because pancake still synchronizes globally between the gate/up and down phases; and a `preserve_thinking` chain that depends on opencode and is not checked end to end. The Chinese expert-prediction literature (Fate, HOBBIT, ProMoE, SP-MoE, SmallThinker) reduces to a single offline recall test, because on PCIe 3.0 the reported gains over LRU are small (**ProMoE: 1.09x on average in llama.cpp**). For sparsity (item 13), Asia brings three additions and no new item: CHESS's per-channel thresholds, sparsity unioned over the verification window (from BlockFFN), and the transposed layout of down. One correction to the earlier plans: the idea "Any-Precision experts for draft steps" does not apply to pancake, because **the MTP drafter has all 512 of its own experts in VRAM (835 MiB)** and does not touch the CPU. All figures are the authors', on NVIDIA hardware, except those from MOD_PLAN and the code checks done today.

## How I read the papers: the CPU decides, then the agent

**Lead** means the first author or the senior group is at the named institution. **Co** means the institution appears only among the co-authors. **[K]** marks figures from the researchers' notes that come from training knowledge and were not re-read in the source in this round; I use them only as orientation. The report covers Tsinghua and Peking, the rest of mainland China (SJTU, SYSU, Wuhan, HIT, USTC, Zhejiang), Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Korea, Japan and India, plus the company labs Qwen/Alibaba, DeepSeek, Moonshot, ByteDance Seed, MiniMax, Xiaomi, Meituan, Zhipu, Huawei, Baidu and Tencent, over Nov. 2022 - Nov. 2026.

The criterion is the one from the previous reports. At decode, the GPU works **15.9 ms of a 24.8 ms window**, and the rest of the window is set by the experts computed on the CPU ([MOD_PLAN](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/MOD_PLAN.md)). A paper counts if it **reduces the bytes read by the CPU**, **hides CPU time under GPU work**, **raises the useful hit rate without moving the cost onto PCIe 3.0** (about 115 µs for a 1.4 MB promotion, against ~42 µs of CPU compute, per MOD_PLAN item 14), or **reduces the agent's wasted tokens and turns**. Pancake already has a tiered expert cache with adaptive tier, miss computation on the CPU, `--pcie-frac 0`, router lookahead, `STRATA_POOL_PREREAD`, MTP + prompt-lookup with GDN replay, `DraftPolicy`, `SuffixDrafter`, grammar in speculation, a conversation cache with checkpoints, per-level thinking budgets, the loop guard and tool-call recovery. **Tested with no gain**: HIP graphs, unroll, Vulkan, TurboQuant KV, L3 prefetch, ClassLRU, reuse-aware admission, DraftExpert windows, the GDN megakernel at decode and `--spec 3/5/6`. Today three ideas were ported on side branches, not merged yet: **DISCO** (`feat/disco`, a learned stopping rule for the MTP chain), **ContextPilot** (`feat/contextpilot`, deduplication of repeated blocks in the prompt) and **BitDecoding HIP** (`feat/bitdecoding-hip`, decode attention on WMMA). The European report had left them out; here they appear as work in progress where an Asian paper touches them.

The main negative finding: **no Asian company lab has published work on expert offload on a single consumer GPU**, nor expert activation statistics for Qwen3-Next or Flash-Next. The work of DeepSeek, ByteDance, Moonshot and Huawei (DeepEP, EPLB, MegaScale-Infer, Comet, Mooncake, 3FS, MoGE) is for clusters and does not transfer. The batch-1 offload literature comes from universities (SJTU, SYSU, Tsinghua, Peking, HKU, NUS, KAIST). From Japan and India I found nothing relevant. The searches do not filter reliably by affiliation, so this is weak evidence of absence, not a certainty.

## What the Qwen report says about our engines

The official model card and the technical report describe a more complicated model than a scaled-up Qwen3-Next. Flash-Next has **125B parameters in experts, 6B active, plus 51B in the n-gram table and 4B in MTP**. The architecture has 48 layers `12 x (3 x (Gated DeltaNet -> MoE) -> 1 x (Qwen Sparse Attention -> MoE))`, with **512 experts, 10 routed + 1 shared**, intermediate size 640, a **4-branch Gated Residual** stream and a single MTP layer "trained with multiple steps" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The technical report gives **a mean accepted length of 4.07 for 4-step speculative decoding** (4.06 with full attention), but the text is inconsistent: the kernel benchmark uses `next_n=4`, described as "three MTP steps" ([alphaXiv 2608.30320](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30320.md)). SGLang reports **540 tok/s at acceptance 3.3 (including the bonus token)** on B200 at batch 1, disables PLE in the draft and reuses the QSA top-k in all draft steps "with accepted length unchanged" ([LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-26-qwen-flash-next)). I compared each point with the pancake source and the Avalon launcher.

**The MTP depth is correct in pancake and too small in Avalon.** The Q2_0 preset asks for 4 proposals per verification, and `--spec-min-p 0.5` stops the chain when the drafter is unsure. This is already a form of adaptive depth, of the kind MiMo ties to entropy. **MiMo-V2-Flash (Xiaomi, lead)** reports **acceptance length of up to 3.6 and 2.6x at decode with 3 MTP layers**, plus the observation that next-token entropy strongly determines acceptance ([arXiv 2601.02780](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02780v1)). MOD_PLAN measured `--spec 3/5/6` flat, which confirms that 4 is close to optimal for the verification cost on this PC: each extra row wakes up the union of the experts of the k+1 tokens. Something can still be gained only in the stopping rule, not in the ceiling. Here DISCO, already on `feat/disco`, is the right mechanism. It uses the top-1 probability, the surprisal, the probability chain, the position, recent acceptance, the prompt-lookup match length, expert misses and the measured cost of an extra row. From Asia it is missing a single feature, **the entropy of the drafter's distribution**, not just the top-1 probability. Avalon is in a different situation: `--spec-draft-n-max 2` leaves half of the depth MTP was trained for unused. Community data (not primary, not comparable) gives acceptance of **94% when emitting code against 50-58% on reasoning text** ([community summary](https://huggingface.co/RockTalk/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-MLX-4bit)), so the gain would be on edit turns. One factor works in our favor: the user runs at T=0.1, and acceptance rises as temperature falls. The official 4.07 figure, measured under evaluation conditions, is probably a lower bound for our traces, not a target.

**The SGLang draft shortcuts already exist in pancake.** The pancake drafter does not compute PLE: in the draft, the table rows are only read ahead on the host, so they are ready when the draft tokens reach verification. The drafter's attention is dense over its own window (a KV ring of `window + 4*max_t + 64` cells), so there is no QSA indexer to reuse. It is the same compromise **DeepSeek-V4** makes with an MTP head that is "SWA-only, no compressor, no indexer" ([LMSYS DeepSeek-V4](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-04-25-deepseek-v4)). The n-gram table path calls for a correction of the European report: it said `mmap` stays correct at 57 GB RAM. In reality, **the default mode is `--ple-io direct`**: unbuffered and asynchronous SSD reads, a row cache of 1,048,576 × 90 B, and prefetch for the draft tokens and for the verification windows ([generate.cpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/src/program/generate.cpp)). On Linux, `prefetch_rows` does something only in direct mode; in `mmap` mode it is a no-op ([ngram.cpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/src/kernels/ngram.cpp)). The `STRATA_PLE_AUTO_RAM` policy correctly refuses to lock the 28.8 GB IQ4_NL shard in RAM next to the arena. The practical conclusion: the default mode is the right one and must not be switched to `mmap`. What is worth doing is a single measurement: the "PLE prefetch" time from `strata mtp draft timing` and the row cache hit rate on a long opencode trace. Deterministic addressing, which Qwen and Engram ([DeepSeek Engram](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/Engram)) use precisely for host offload, is already exploited.

**Gated Residual: FP8 does not help at batch 1, fusion does.** Qwen says that storing the residual stream in FP8 "halves the bytes moved with almost no loss" and names memory traffic as the dominant cost of GR ([alphaXiv 2608.30320](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30320.md)). For us the math looks different. The stream has 4 × 2560 values, that is 40 KB in FP32 per token and per block. Over 48 blocks, with one read and one write each, that is ~4 MB per token, a few microseconds at the 7900 XT's bandwidth. Qwen's claim is about large batches. At batch 1, the ~2.7 ms of hyper-connection reads in item 1 are latency and kernel launches, not bytes. Pancake already has a Q8 read of the stream and fused GR kernels. The useful lesson comes from SGLang: **the "small-M" kernels for M=4 (Mix 2.05x, Combine 1.96x) brought +7.6% and +5.49% end to end** ([LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-26-qwen-flash-next)). M=4 is exactly the size of our verification windows. In the item 1 rocprofv3 profile, count the launches per block for Mix/Combine. The target: a single pass in each direction, as Qwen describes. RDNA3 has no native FP8 anyway.

**`preserve_thinking` is the chain most likely to be broken.** Qwen keeps it on by default, so the model was trained to see the thinking of earlier turns. The Avalon template keeps `reasoning_content` when the variable is missing, and the pancake server renders the official model template. That leaves opencode: the local provider configuration declares nothing about resending reasoning ([opencode.jsonc](file:///home/dan/.config/opencode/opencode.jsonc)), and for a manually declared `openai-compatible` model it is possible that `reasoning_content` is not sent back. If it is lost, two problems appear. Behavior deviates from training. Worse for speed, **the conversation prefix diverges at the first removed thinking block**, and the conversation cache re-reads everything from there. The check is cheap: the rendered prompt at the third turn of an opencode session, compared with the tokens in the cache. If the `<think>` blocks are missing, add the interleaved reasoning field to the opencode model definition (for example `interleaved`, per the schema of the installed version). MOD_PLAN item 16 already made the checkpoint after `</think>` conditional on this.

**Sampling and effort: official values reported, the user's choice kept.** For thinking, the card gives **T=1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, min_p 0, presence_penalty 0**. The agentic coding benchmarks ran at T=1.0 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The Qwen3 report discouraged greedy decoding because of repetition ([arXiv 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388), [K]). The user explicitly runs T=0.1, and the loop guard taken from Maya covers exactly the repetition risk. I do not recommend changing it. `presence_penalty` must stay 0, as MOD_PLAN already required after the DASLab card. The real tension is in effort. Qwen states that **lower effort does not always shorten the total task in multi-turn agentic use, because it causes more failures and retries** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The Stanford, MIT/Harvard and US (A2) reports recommended `low`/`medium` by default with escalation on failure. The two positions do not contradict each other in principle: one measures time per turn, the other time per task. The resolution is a measurement (M5 below), of total time to success on the same tasks, at `xhigh` against `medium`.

**Global expert balancing favors per-session pinning.** The Qwen paper "Demons in the Detail" shows that **global-batch load balancing "greatly improves the domain specialization of experts"** ([arXiv 2501.11873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11873v2)). SmallThinker (SJTU IPADS) uses the same mechanism precisely so that per-task working sets stay small and easy to cache ([arXiv 2507.20984](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20984v1)). For a single-user coding session, this means a concentrated expert set with slow drift, which is exactly the premise of items 3-4 (`STRATA_CONV_WORKSET`, `STRATA_DOMAIN_PROFILES`). It is not a new item. It is one more reason to collect the per-session traces that items 3-4 need.

## KTransformers changes the rule in item 8, not the mechanism

**KTransformers (Tsinghua MADSys lead, Approaching.AI co, SOSP '25)** is the only Asian systems paper with a mechanism that transfers directly to the 5700G. Expert Deferral splits a layer's routed experts into two groups. The best by score, for example the top-2, feed layer k+1 directly. The rest run on the CPU while the GPU computes the attention of layer k+1, and their result is then added to the residual. **Deferral does not apply to the last layer.** On DeepSeek-V3, deferring 3 experts cut the time per layer by 26% and raised decode by 33%; overall, the gain reaches **"up to 45%"**, with tasks moving by at most 2 points ([SOSP'25 PDF](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf)). The paper also notes that deferral is counterproductive at prefill, where almost all experts are active and the memory footprint doubles.

Pancake has more than the notes assumed. `STRATA_VERIFY_DEFER_CPU=1` defers the CPU rows of layer l until just before the FFN read of layer l+1. `STRATA_VERIFY_DEFER_CPU_BELOW` (default 0.1) makes the pool compute first the CPU rows with routing weight ≥ the threshold, and the deferring layer combines those on time and the light ones late. **The last layer of the stage is never deferred**, and layer 0 is not deferred when it carries the PLE block ([verify.hpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/include/strata/core/verify.hpp), [verify.cpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/src/core/verify.cpp)). The difference from KTransformers is the selection rule. KTransformers guarantees **a rank**: the top-2 are always immediate. Pancake guarantees **a weight**: everything above 0.1 is immediate. With top-10 of 512 and renormalized weights, the first two usually exceed 0.1, but not always. On tokens with flat scores, a top-1 below the threshold would be deferred, and KTransformers' accuracy evidence (≤2 points) covers only score-ordered deferral. The recommendation is a rank guard next to the threshold, of the form "immediate if in the top-2 **or** weight ≥ w". The test matrix: windows of 1, 2, 4 and 8 tokens and w ∈ {0.1; 0.2}, with the token-forcing gate. Large windows look like prefill, and there the gain should drop.

The order matters, though: **item 8 still gives non-finite logits on HIP with the ported kernels** (MOD_PLAN), and the bisect is the first step. The hand-off protocol is already equivalent to the one in KTransformers. There, `cudaLaunchHostFunc` and a lock-free queue keep the host uninterrupted in the captured graph. Pancake has doorbells, a partial flag (`h_pflag_`) and `WAIT_FUSE`. HIP graphs were tested with no gain, so there is nothing to port here. My estimate, starting from the authors' DS-V3 profile and from the 8.9 ms tied to the CPU above the GPU ceiling in MOD_PLAN: **+10-25% at decode** after the fix, lossy, opt-in.

The second Tsinghua paper, **"Achieving Cloud-Grade SLOs for Local MoE Inference" (Tsinghua lead, OSDI '26)**, brings a small and exact idea. Each gate/up GEMV is cut into per-expert segments, **per-expert barriers replace the global barrier**, and the combine is fused into the first stage ([USENIX PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi26-wang-wenxin.pdf)). I checked the pancake pool: `run_phase` synchronizes globally between the gate/up phase of all missed experts and the down phase ([pool.cpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/src/kernels/cpu/pool.cpp)). The authors already softened the tail with about three tasks per thread. A per-expert dependency would let threads start the down of expert e as soon as its gate/up is done. The gain is small, only on the synchronization part of CPU time. I estimate 1-4% of decode, exact. The other papers in the same family are already covered by what pancake has or by what was tested. **HybriMoE (Peking lead, DAC 2025)** reports 1.70x at decode over a kTransformers without adaptive cache ([arXiv 2504.05897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897)), but its score-based cache resembles ClassLRU, and the dynamic CPU/GPU split resembles `--pcie-frac`. **AdapMoE (Peking lead)** and **MoBiLE (Tsinghua lead)** reduce the number of experts for insensitive tokens (1.60-1.72x on consumer GPUs, per [arXiv 2510.12357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12357)). They are the principled version of `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR` and `turbo` and fall under the same quality gates.

## Expert prediction reduces to an offline test

The best batch-1 evidence comes from **ProMoE and HOBBIT (SJTU lead)** and from **Fate and SP-MoE (Sun Yat-sen lead)**. All use cross-layer prediction: the gate input of layer l, or the hidden state of a draft, passed through the router of layer l+1…l+3. They report **84.7-97% accuracy on the next layer**, but on models with 8-64 experts. The measured gain over LRU is modest. **ProMoE in llama.cpp: 1.49x over a static cache, but only 1.09x on average (max 1.17x) over LRU**, on PCIe 4.0. On Mixtral, llama.cpp's CPU offload beats ProMoE at decode, because computing on the CPU was cheaper than transferring over PCIe ([arXiv 2410.22134](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22134v3)). **HOBBIT** ("stacking": the current gate input through the next p gates) gives ~96% top-1 on the next layer and ~90% at 2-3 layers, but reduces the miss penalty by only **4.69-8.68% against LRU** ([arXiv 2411.01433](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01433v2)). **Fate** reports 97.15% accuracy and ~99% hit with a cache that favors early layers ([arXiv 2502.12224](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12224v2)), figures from the abstract that I did not check in the full text. **SP-MoE** passes the attention output of each draft layer through the gate of the target layer (~88-89% top-1). It also shows a U-shaped curve: **too much prefetching adds I/O contention** ([arXiv 2510.10302](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10302v2)). **Pre-gated MoE (KAIST lead, ISCA 2024)** needs gate retraining ([arXiv 2308.12066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12066v3)), so it stays only as a precedent. **SmallThinker (SJTU IPADS)** puts the router before attention through training ([arXiv 2507.20984](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20984v1)). Pancake cannot retrain, but it can test the signal for free.

On PCIe 3.0 with CPU compute, most of these gains vanish. A Flash-Next expert has 3 × 2560 × 640 ≈ 4.9M parameters, so ~1.3-1.4 MB at ~2 bits. A promotion costs ~115 µs against ~42 µs of CPU compute (MOD_PLAN item 14). A hit point gained through uncovered promotions can cost more than it saves. The existing replay (tier 79.6%, LRU 89.5%, Belady 94.7%) assumes free promotions, and item 14 already asks for a time-cost version. Pancake already has router lookahead for the next layer and `STRATA_POOL_PREREAD`, so what is worth testing are the signals it does not have. I propose **a single offline recall test on the routing traces**, with recall@10/16/24 on the real top-10 for three predictors: (a) the pre-MoE state of layer l through routers l+1 and **l+2** (Fate/HOBBIT); (b) the **pre-attention** state through the same layer's router (the SmallThinker signal, with one extra block of lead); (c) **the MTP drafter's hidden state** through the main routers, for the experts verification will request (the SP-MoE analog). With 512 experts, recall will be well below 90-97%. The threshold for continuing: recall@16 ≥ 80% **and** a gain ≥ 5% of decode in the item 14 simulator with PCIe cost.

If the test passes, two ProMoE mechanisms deserve porting regardless of the predictor, because they cost no accuracy. The first is **chunked transfer with early preemption**: one chunk per matrix, so that an urgent transfer waits at most one chunk, and a post-gate hook cancels the layer's remaining speculative prefetches. The second is **reordered execution**: cached experts run first, then those in flight, then the rest, which is legal because the sum of experts does not depend on order. Pancake already runs the GPU and the CPU in parallel, so the second mechanism must be checked in the code before it is considered new. **BuddyMoE (SJTU lead, PPoPP 2026)** substitutes a resident "sibling" expert on a miss. It loses ~5% on ARC ([arXiv 2511.10054](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10054v1)) and overlaps with `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR`. It is useful only as a source for the substitution table, through conditional co-activation. **SpecMoE (KAIST lead, DAC 2026)** drafts only with hot experts and affinity remapping ([alphaXiv 2604.10152](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.10152.md)). Its gains are at large batch and resemble the DraftExpert windows, already tested. In pancake, anyway, the draft does not touch the CPU.

## Fewer bytes on the CPU: three additions to item 13, one correction on mixed precision

Activation sparsity remains the lever with the highest ceiling for the CPU half of decoding. Asia, however, adds only refinements, not new methods. **ProSparse, Sparsing Law and BlockFFN (THUNLP lead)** need ReLU-fication or pre-training ([arXiv 2402.13516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13516), [arXiv 2411.02335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02335), [arXiv 2507.08771](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08771v2)), so they do not apply to Flash-Next's SwiGLU experts. Two results from them still transfer. First: **BlockFFN keeps ~80% sparsity at token level, but only ~71% unioned over blocks of 8 tokens**, even though it was trained for locality. Pancake computes CPU experts mostly in MTP verification windows, so the per-token sparsity from TEAL or Berkeley overestimates the bytes saved. The item 13 offline pass must also report **the sparsity unioned over w = 1, 2, 4, 8**, and that costs only one extra column. Second: the **CETT-PPL-1%** metric from Sparsing Law gives a per-layer threshold that can check TEAL's greedy allocation. **CHESS (Wuhan lead, with CityU HK)** replaces the single threshold in CATS with **per-channel thresholds**, loses less quality at fewer active parameters and reaches 1.27x end to end with sparse CPU kernels ([arXiv 2409.01366](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.01366v2)). It is calibrated without training and is a cheap fourth mask for the same offline pass. The third addition comes from PowerInfer (SJTU IPADS, SOSP 2024) read against Q2: **Q2 blocks run along the input dimension**, so skipping columns in down needs a transposed layout (n_ff-major), planned before the study. Otherwise the measured sparsity does not turn into bandwidth. MOD_PLAN item 13 already provides for the repack at load time. Asia only confirms that without it there is no gain ([arXiv 2312.12456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12456)).

On mixed precision, Asia has the most papers: **MC-MoE (HKU lead, ICLR 2025)**, with 2.54 bits on average, per-expert bits by frequency, score and error, plus dynamic pruning to top-1 for unimportant tokens (−15% active parameters at <0.6% loss, per [arXiv 2410.06270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06270)); **HOBBIT**, which loads low-weight missed experts in low precision (cumulative thresholds 0.6/0.9, top-1 always high precision, ≤1% on GSM8K); and **MxMoE (ICML 2025)**. For Q2_0, they all hit the same wall: at 2 bits there is no room left to go lower for the cold tail. They are relevant only if the daily file moves to one with more bits, such as the ~3.5 bpw Coder variant from the European report. Here is also the announced **correction**. **Any-Precision LLM (SNU lead, ICML 2024)** stores weights in bitplanes, so a single copy serves several bit widths ([arXiv 2402.10517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10517)). The notes proposed reading 3 of 4 bitplanes "for draft steps" with exact verification. In pancake, however, **the MTP drafter has all 512 of its own experts in VRAM: "835 MiB of VRAM (experts 675, dense 111)"** ([pancake log](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/pancake-flashnext.log), [mtp.cpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/src/core/mtp.cpp)). The draft reads no CPU experts, and verification must stay exact. On Q2_0, Any-Precision has nowhere to save. It would make sense only for the low-weight tail on a 3-4 bit file, as a HOBBIT-style lossy variant. **T-MAC (USTC lead, with MSRA)** does 2-bit GEMV through lookup tables, up to 6.6x faster than the 2024 llama.cpp kernels ([arXiv 2407.00088](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00088v2)). MOD_PLAN notes, however, that **the IQ2_S kernels are compute-bound** (`STRATA_IQ2S_PAIR` neutral), so a micro-benchmark of the Q2_0 AVX2 kernel against DDR4 would immediately show whether T-MAC has room. **ERNIE 4.5 (Baidu)** argues that serving MoE at ~2 bits is viable ([report](https://yiyan.baidu.com/blog/publication/ERNIE_Technical_Report.pdf), [K]), which Q2_0 already does.

## Long attention, verification and the agent

**BFLA (CityU lead, arXiv 2605.12193)** is the only paper found that **directly measures Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**. Training-free sparse prefill, with an indexer over FP8 key blocks, gives **1.342-1.435x TTFT at 128-256K** with no loss: LongBench-v2 57.32 against 57.32 dense, Terminal Bench 2.1 86.89 against 86.52 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12193)). The gain is small against ~4x on dense models, because it applies only to the 12 attention layers. The kernels are FA3/Hopper, and the measurements were on H200. For pancake, BFLA is a better candidate than XAttention for item 19, because it has evidence on our model and on an agent benchmark. It still stays behind the M5 measurement (decode and prefill at 10k against 200k). The preset context is 131,072 anyway, and QSA shows gains only from 64K. The conversation cache removes most repeated prefills from opencode. **SageAttention/SpargeAttn (Tsinghua ML lead)** exist only in CUDA ([arXiv 2411.10958](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10958v3)) and have lower priority than BFLA. **NSA (Peking lead, with DeepSeek)**, **InfLLM-V2 (Tsinghua/OpenBMB)**, **MoBA** and **Kimi Linear** need training. **LongSpec (NTU Singapore lead, ACL 2025)** contributes a kernel idea. **Hybrid Tree Attention** passes the prefix through ordinary attention and the speculative block through a small masked kernel, then combines them through log-sum-exp, with **~75% less attention latency** than the HF implementation ([arXiv 2502.17421](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17421)). Pancake already uses split-K decode kernels with merge for the verification windows. One audit remains: whether the T rows of a window read the prefix KV **only once**, not T times. This ties directly to the BitDecoding port (`feat/bitdecoding-hip`), which fills the M dimension of the WMMA tile with the GQA heads and could also pack the window rows there.

For the rest of speculation, the Asian verdict is negative. **SWIFT (PolyU lead)** and **CAS-Spec** skip layers, which breaks the GDN recurrent state. A study from outside Asia measured **α = 0.038 at k = 2 on sequential Qwen3.5 hybrids** ([arXiv 2605.01106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01106)). **EAGLE-3**, **SpecBlock (HKUST lead)** and **GRIFFIN** need a trained drafter. **AdaSD (Academia Sinica/NTU Taiwan)** relaxes acceptance, so it is lossy ([arXiv 2512.11280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11280)). **Token Recycling (HIT lead, ACL 2025)** is a third training-free drafter, with an adjacency matrix under 2 MB and ~2x on SpecBench ([arXiv 2408.08696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08696)). MTP, however, covers the no-match case, and each extra draft widens the union of experts; if it were tested, it should first be simulated in `--simulate`. The closest design reference for GDN replay is **SpecLA (Nanjing/Alibaba)**: it recovers accepted states from factors produced at verification, instead of replaying ([arXiv 2607.16673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16673)). It goes together with the ReplaySSM audit in item 20.

On the agent side, the cheapest lesson is already followed. **"Let Me Speak Freely?" (NTU Taiwan co, Appier lead)** shows that JSON/XML formats enforced during reasoning lower quality ([arXiv 2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)). Pancake's grammar guard **defers the grammar until the thinking block closes** ([guard.hpp](file:///home/dan/clone-of-d/src/Strata/include/strata/program/grammar/guard.hpp)). Item 26, with the ETH constraints, must keep this property. **AutoCodeRover and SpecRover (NUS lead)** use AST-based code search (classes, methods) instead of whole files ([arXiv 2404.05427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05427)). **ContextSniper (Huawei lead, NTU Singapore co)** filters long tool outputs before they enter the context: **51.5% fewer tokens** on SWE-bench Lite ([arXiv 2607.01916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01916)). Both cut per-turn prefill, which on a local engine costs more than on an API. They combine with ContextPilot (`feat/contextpilot`), which deduplicates repeated blocks in the server. **AutoCompact (NTU Singapore/SMU lead)** learns when to compact and what working state to keep ([arXiv 2610.02163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163)). Training is beyond our means, but the design lesson applies: compaction keeps the touched files, the failing test and the plan. Each compaction invalidates the conversation cache. **AdaptThink (Tsinghua KEG lead)** and **Thinkless (NUS lead)** show that the model can learn to skip thinking on easy problems ([arXiv 2505.13417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13417), [arXiv 2505.13379](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13379)). The inference-time equivalent is a zero budget on mechanical turns, and item 21 (the learned probe) is its natural place. **KVzip (SNU, NeurIPS 2025)** compresses KV by 3-4x independently of the query ([arXiv 2505.23416](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23416v1.pdf)). It is lossy, and **KVFundaBench (HKUST-GZ lead)** shows that reasoning degrades much faster than retrieval under KV compression ([arXiv 2502.01941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01941)). It stays parked, in line with the TurboQuant null result.

The order follows a precise logic. P1 is the only Asian idea with a double-digit ceiling, but it is blocked by the HIP bisect, so it starts with the fix, not with the new rule. P2 and C1 cost almost nothing and can directly change the daily experience: one cuts hidden re-prefills, the other aligns Avalon with the depth MTP was trained for. P3 and M3 feed each other on the same traces. P4 and P5 are small but exact gains, which the item 1 profile will confirm or refute. M1, M2 and M6 are not projects, but extra columns in measurements already planned, added so that nothing is built on figures from PCIe 4.0 with 8 experts. ## Conclusion

So far the series has looked for outside ideas for the engine. The Asian report is the first that could be checked in the opposite direction: I took the specification from the people who trained the model and checked line by line whether the engine follows it. Pancake passes the test almost completely, and does a few things by another route than SGLang, with the same effect (a drafter with a dense window instead of a reused QSA top-k). The real deviations are not in the engine. They are at the edges: an Avalon setting left over from an old configuration, and a client-template-cache chain that nobody has followed end to end. Two lessons remain for the rest of the series. The cheapest check is the one between the model's documentation and our own settings. And an efficiency claim made for large batches, such as FP8 on the residual stream, loses its meaning at batch 1.

The second conclusion concerns the offload literature. Asia has produced almost all of the batch-1 MoE literature, but almost all of it on PCIe 4.0, with 8-64 experts and without CPU compute. On our machine, the same ideas are either already in pancake or lose their gain to the cost of a promotion. What survives on this hardware is not prediction but overlap: the KTransformers deferral hides CPU time under GPU work, instead of trying to avoid it. This moves the focus from the expert cache, where three reports found almost nothing, to the order in which CPU and GPU work finishes inside the window. That is where the remaining ceiling is.
