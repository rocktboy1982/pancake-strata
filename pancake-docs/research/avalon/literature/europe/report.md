# ISTA and Imperial change the order of the pancake list

The most useful European papers for pancake are the very ones that produced the daily model. **ISTA-DASLab (lead)** built the Qwen3.8-Flash-Next GSQ-RCO files with two methods of its own: GSQ, learned scalar quantization, and RCO, per-tensor allocation of GGUF types under an exact bit budget. Their model card shows that **Q2_0 decodes at 93.8 tok/s against 70.3 for IQ2_XS, with only a 0.09-point loss on the task average**. From the same place we learn that the "IQ2_XS" file actually contains no IQ2_XS tensor, that the base model has **176.9B parameters** (not ~125B, as our notes say), and that there is a **Coder variant** with 256 of the 512 experts and only 29.6 GB resident. On hardware, the second most important paper is **AcceptMoE (Imperial College London, probable lead)**. It narrows the set of experts touched when verifying drafts, preferring experts already in the cache: **2.06x under offload and −0.27 accuracy points**. It is the verification analog of the existing `turbo` preset. **Cache-conditional routing (Qualcomm AI Research Amsterdam)** externally validates `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR`, but it is not a new item. What remains is to finish the token-forcing check (MOD_PLAN item 7). The rest of Europe brings extra evidence for activation sparsity (Warsaw/Sapienza, hence item 13), type-constrained decoding and agent benchmarks (ETH SRI and SPY Lab), and a security warning for third-party GGUF files ("Mind the Gap", ETH). I checked one practical consequence locally: in the IQ2_XS file on disk, **3 layers have gate/up in IQ1_M**, a type that pancake's native AVX2 kernels do not handle and leave on the ggml-cpu path. This is one more argument for Q2_0. All figures are the authors', on other hardware, except those from MOD_PLAN and the local file inspection.

## How I read the papers: the CPU decides, not the GPU kernel

**Lead** means the first author or the senior group is at the named institution. **Co** means the institution appears only among the co-authors. **Unconfirmed** means the affiliation was not visible on the page I read. The report covers ETH Zürich, EPFL, ISTA, the British universities (Edinburgh, Imperial, Cambridge, Oxford, UCL), Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Czechia, France (Inria), Italy (Sapienza), Spain (BSC/UPC, Cantabria), Portugal (IST Lisbon), the Nordic countries (KTH), Poland (Warsaw/IDEAS NCBR) and Israel (Weizmann, Hebrew U, Technion, Tel Aviv), over Nov. 2022 - Nov. 2026. Qualcomm AI Research Amsterdam and Intel Labs Israel are company labs. I included them because their papers have university co-authors or directly touch our mechanism, and where relevant I mark them as such.

The selection criterion is the one from the report on the rest of the US. At decode, the GPU works **15.9 ms of a 24.8 ms window**, and the rest of the window is set by the experts computed on the CPU, close to the DDR4 limit ([MOD_PLAN](../../../../../MOD_PLAN.md)). A paper counts only if it does at least one of three things: **reduces the bytes read by the CPU**, **raises the useful hit rate** without moving the cost onto PCIe 3.0, or **reduces the agent's wasted tokens and turns**. Pancake already has a tiered expert cache with adaptive tier, miss computation on the CPU, `--pcie-frac` (best at 0), router lookahead (the same choices for the next layer, computed ahead), MTP + prompt-lookup with GDN replay, `DraftPolicy` (choice and length at each step), `SuffixDrafter`, grammar inside speculation, a conversation cache with checkpoints at boundaries, per-level thinking budgets with soft close, tool-call recovery and the Maya loop guard. **Tested with no gain**: HIP graphs, unroll, Vulkan, TurboQuant KV, L3 prefetch, ClassLRU scoring, reuse-aware admission, DraftExpert windows and the GDN megakernel at decode. Replay shows tier 79.6%, LRU 89.5% and Belady 94.7% at 10,909 cached experts. Nothing from the "tested" list is recommended again without a new reason.

The main negative finding: **no ETH or EPFL paper covers expert offload, caching or prefetching on a single node**. The same holds for TUM, KIT, Darmstadt, Delft, Leuven, TU Wien, Inria, BSC and the Nordic, Greek and Romanian groups. The European literature on batch-1 MoE offload comes from Edinburgh (MoE-Infinity) and Imperial (AcceptMoE), and the rest is Asian. Web searches do not filter reliably by affiliation, so this is weak evidence that such papers are missing, not a certainty.

## ISTA-DASLab explains the daily files: Q2_0 stays, Coder needs an A/B

The daily files are **GSQ-RCO** builds, not classic GPTQ, AQLM or HIGGS. GSQ ([arXiv 2604.18556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18556), ISTA lead) jointly learns, through a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation, the assignment of each coordinate on the grid and the per-group scales. The output stays in symmetric per-group scalar formats, so ordinary GGUF, and the method closes most of the gap to QTIP and PV-Tuning: at 2.13 bpp on Llama-3.1-70B, **75.57 against 77.25 (QTIP) and 57.38 (GPTQ)** ([GSQ README](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/GSQ)). RCO ([arXiv 2605.00649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00649), ISTA lead) treats the bit budget as a smooth manifold in logit space and respects it exactly at each step. For each layer it picks a GGUF type, because GGUF cannot express different types per expert ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF)). The practical conclusion is that **requantizing at home is not realistic**. GSQ needs 8x H200 for Qwen3.5-122B and 16x for 397B, and the base BF16 checkpoint is **354 GB**, against 78 GB free on D ([Coder card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Coder-GGUF)).

The DASLab card confirms the Q2_0 decision from the outside (MOD_PLAN item 6). In llama.cpp, on 55 prompts from 11 categories, **Q2_0 does 367.5 tok/s at prompt and 93.8 at decode, and IQ2_XS 108.2 and 70.3**. Q2_0 decode stays between 92.5 and 94.5 tok/s in all categories, while IQ2_XS swings between 49.4 and 94.5, "because the lookup cost varies". The quality difference on the task average is 89.07 against 89.16 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF)). The published allocation files show that in the "IQ2_XS" build **gate/up_exps are IQ2_S in 34 layers, IQ2_XXS in 11 and IQ1_M in 3, and down_exps are Q2_0 everywhere** ([IQ2_XS allocation](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF/raw/main/tensor-allocation/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-IQ2_XS-00001-of-00002.rco-allocation.txt)). In the Q2_0 build, all 144 routed expert tensors are Q2_0 ([Q2_0 allocation](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF/raw/main/tensor-allocation/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Q2_0-00001-of-00002.rco-allocation.txt)). The cause is a format constraint: `ffn_down_exps` rows have width 640, which is not divisible by 256, so they rule out all K and I types and leave only Q2_0 (block 64) or IQ4_NL (block 32).

I read the header of the IQ2_XS file on D with gguf-py and found exactly the same distribution: **3 layers have `ffn_gate_exps`/`ffn_up_exps` in IQ1_M**, and `expert_count` is 512. In the pancake source, `src/kernels/cpu/iq_avx2.cpp` says "IQ1_M stays on ggml-cpu". The comment in `iq_avx512.cpp` even claims that no shipped model has IQ1_M expert rows, which the real file contradicts. Therefore, in the IQ2_XS presets, misses in those 3 layers go through ggml-cpu's generic dot product, not through the native kernel. The real cost must be measured (for example with `cpu_dispatch_dump`). My estimate, not measured: 1-4% at decode on IQ2_XS. On Q2_0 the cost disappears completely. The other idea in the notes for Q2_0, the product `Σ(q·a) − 2·Σa` with `maddubs`, **already exists**: `q2_avx2.cpp` explicitly subtracts the −1 code offset through a per-chunk `hx` and uses `vpdpbusd` where AVX-VNNI exists.

The **Coder variant** ([card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Coder-GGUF), released in Oct. 2026) is the most relevant new file for an opencode agent. RCO chose the **256 experts kept per layer** by minimizing the whole model's KL on code, agentic tool-use and vision data. The result is **79.20 on SWE-bench Verified against 82.80 for BF16 (95.7%)** and 87.43 on LCB v6, on par with BF16. The Oct. 17 update raised SWE-bench from 75.60 and "reduced looping". Shard 1 is **29.6 GB**, against 37.6 GB for Q2_0, but the kept experts are at ~3.5 bpw in lookup formats (IQ2_S, IQ3_XXS, IQ3_S on gate/up, IQ4_NL on down) ([Coder allocation](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Coder-GGUF/raw/main/tensor-allocation/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-IQ1_M-00001-of-00002.rco-allocation.txt)). The net effect on pancake is hard to predict. In favor of Coder: more experts fit in VRAM and in the RAM arena, and NVMe leaves the critical path. Against: each CPU miss reads ~1.4-1.5x more bytes, and the IQ formats are exactly the ones DASLab shows to be slower. My estimate is between −10% and +20% at decode. Pancake's native kernels already cover IQ2_S/IQ3_XXS/IQ3_S and the IQ4_NL down rows, and `iq_avx512.cpp` mentions the "Coder IQ1_M" pack by name, so format support seems to exist. Three things must be checked: `expert_count` 256 in the metadata, the per-expert layout in the allocation, and the fact that **the learned expert profile (8.6) and the seeds of items 3-4 must be regenerated**, because the expert IDs change. The card states that **shard 2 (the 28.8 GB IQ4_NL n-gram table) is identical in all variants**. If the LFS hash matches the shard already downloaded, only 29.6 GB need to be fetched. Watch the disk budget: MOD_PLAN notes that the Avalon model also still needs ~20 GB on D. The best possible outcome would be a "Coder Q2_0". The Coder downs are already only Q2_0/IQ4_NL, so DASLab could build it, but a request on the HF forum is a public post and the decision belongs to the owner.

The rest of the DASLab portfolio splits in two. Worth adopting is **EAR (Expected Acceptance Rate)** from SLQ ([code](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/SLQ), COLM 2026, ISTA lead). SLQ defines "distribution-lossless" as EAR ≥0.99 against BF16 and shows that zero-shot accuracy stays flat as long as KL is below ~0.01. EAR measures exactly what matters for MTP: the probability that a token from the quantized model is accepted by the reference one. Put next to "80.6% same top token", it is the natural metric for Q2_0, Coder and any lossy item. Also worth noting is a remark on the MoESQ card: for tool calling, **`presence_penalty` must stay 0**, otherwise SWE-bench drops from 74.7% to 70.6% and missing tool arguments almost triple ([MoESQ card](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-P48NVFP4-MoESQ)). The rest brings no runtime gain on gfx1100 and AVX2: MoESQ (NVFP4 + 4:8 sparsity on Blackwell), Marlin and qutlass (NVIDIA), QMoE (Switch only), AQLM/PV-Tuning (large lookups on AVX2), HIGGS (surpassed by GSQ), GPTQ-2D and WUSH (quantization time only, and W+A FP4 respectively). Disaggregated Quantization ([README](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/disaggregated-quantization)) has a llama.cpp fork, but the gain needs a trained NVFP4 prefiller, and prompts under 4K get slower. MatGPTQ ([arXiv 2602.03537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03537v1)) is the only truly new idea for tiers: a "matryoshka" file would keep the 4-bit slice in VRAM and read only the upper bits from RAM for the CPU experts. There is, however, no MoE GGUF of this kind, and the idea needs new kernels. It is long-term.

As for shard 2, DASLab recommends `-lm mmap --lazy-mode on` and says RAM residency "completely removes the paging cost". Pancake already has the `STRATA_PLE_AUTO_RAM` policy (`ple_residency_test.cpp`), which refuses the table when it does not fit next to the arena. With 57 GB RAM, neither the Q2_0 arena nor the Coder one (29.6 + 28.8 GB) leaves room for the table, so `mmap` stays correct and there is nothing to do.

### Third-party GGUF files: official source and hash, no panic

"Mind the Gap" ([arXiv 2505.23786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23786), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/egashira25a.html), ETH SRI lead, ICML 2025) shows that the quantization error interval leaves room for a model trained to be benign in full precision and malicious only after GGUF quantization. The attack success differences are **88.7% for insecure code**, 85.0% for content injection and 30.1% for refusal, on 3 LLMs and 9 GGUF types. The attack requires the publisher of the model to be malicious and to have trained it specially. The official ISTA-DASLab files do not fall into this category. The real risk appears with community quantizations of unknown origin, like the one used in an Avalon bench on 2026-11-02 (`community-cpu-qwen3-8-flash-next-avalon-iq3ks`). For a coding agent that runs shell commands, the "insecure code" outcome is the most relevant. The recommendation is simple: models only from the official repository, SHA-256 hash compared with the LFS metadata on HF, and keeping the `.rco-allocation.txt` files as a witness of the composition. Community files are used only for benchmarks, not for the daily agent.

## Cache-conditional routing validates `turbo`; AcceptMoE moves the lesson into verification

**Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts** ([arXiv 2412.00099](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099v2), Qualcomm AI Research Amsterdam lead, NeurIPS 2024 demo) starts from the observation that current MoEs have little temporal locality, so LRU thrashes. Their solution is a training-free locality prior in the router that prefers experts already in the cache: **up to 2x against ordinary routing with LRU**, on 4- and 8-bit Qwen-MoE, on phones with 10-16 GB. It is practically pancake's `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR`: `verify.cpp` re-picks the top-10 with a logit bonus for resident experts, opt-in through the `turbo` preset. On the 3060 it gave **+25% at decode, with 2.9% differing tokens (against 1.2-1.3% for stock)**. On the 7900 XT, round A measured **149.3 tok/s against 111.7 for the baseline** ([MOD_PLAN](../../../../../MOD_PLAN.md)), and the final `turbo-hip` preset reaches ~168 tok/s per the measurement in the session. The Qualcomm paper adds no new mechanism. It adds an independent validation and a limit: quality was evaluated only on perplexity, MMLU and GSM8K, never on code, on a model with 512 experts or on a DeltaNet hybrid. The first token forcing on the 7900 XT came out **INCONCLUSIVE (+0.4 points, 0 large-margin events)**. The correct action is to finish item 7: token forcing on longer opencode traces, then the BFCL v4 multi-turn and LiveCodeBench gates for two values of λ. Only after that is it decided whether `turbo-hip` becomes the default for certain modes.

**AcceptMoE** ([alphaXiv 2608.02989](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.02989), Imperial College London, Wayne Luk's group, with Tile-AI; Imperial is the probable lead, but the author-affiliation mapping was not visible) tackles the complementary problem. Verifying a draft activates the **union** of the experts from all positions. AcceptMoE narrows the set: it weights each expert by the estimated acceptance probability of the tokens that request it, sets the size of the set from the request entropy (no manual budget) and, under offload, replaces non-resident experts with cached ones. In SGLang, at batch 1, it reports **1.29x over EAGLE-3 with all experts resident, 2.06x under offload, host-device traffic −73.6 to −77.1%, hit rate +10.8 to +14.2 points and −0.27 accuracy points** on 12 model-task pairs (Qwen3-30B, GPT-OSS-120B; GSM8K, MATH500, HumanEval, MBPP). I found no code.

For pancake, the link to `turbo` is direct, and the difference is in which positions the bonus applies to. `turbo` applies the same λ to all positions of the verification window, including the token that will surely be accepted. AcceptMoE suggests a **depth-graded prior**: λ = 0 at position 0, so the guaranteed token stays exact, and λ rising with depth, weighted by the acceptance that `DraftPolicy` already measures per bucket. Where a deep draft will be rejected anyway, replacing a non-resident expert costs nothing in the output. Where it will be accepted, the cost is small, as much as the logit margin allows. The result should sit between `fast-hip` and `turbo`: fewer differing tokens than 2.9% and part of the +25% gain. My estimate, not measured, is 5-15% over `fast-hip`. The risk is the DraftExpert precedent, but the mechanism differs. DraftExpert changed the **window length** based on misses, and the measurement showed the existing policy was already optimal. AcceptMoE changes the **verifier's expert set** at a fixed length. It stays lossy and opt-in, with the same quality gates as `turbo`.

The Israeli speculation papers do not add much. DISCO ([arXiv 2405.04304](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04304v5), Intel Labs Israel lead, Weizmann and Hebrew U co) picks the draft length with a classifier at each step and gains ~10% over a well-tuned static length. That is exactly what `DraftPolicy` already does. DSI ([ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b36554b97da741b1c48c9de05c73993e-Abstract-Conference.html), Weizmann lead) needs several instances of the target model. Speculation with heterogeneous vocabularies ([arXiv 2502.05202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05202v3), Weizmann + Intel) assumes a separate drafter, which we do not use. Sapienza's Jacobi decoding ([HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2305.10427)) touches the union of experts over the whole block, exactly what AcceptMoE tries to avoid.

## Sparsity and rotation: Europe strengthens item 13 and postpones QuaRot

For activation sparsity, Europe brings no new item, but evidence for item 13. **Universal Properties of Activation Sparsity in Modern LLMs** ([arXiv 2509.00454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00454v1), [ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/ec3c79dc0c2b85532cfd1012a4aaa923-Abstract-Conference.html); Szatkowski's group, Warsaw/IDEAS NCBR lead unconfirmed, Sapienza co) describes universal patterns of robustness to sparsification for non-ReLU activations and shows that **sparsity potential grows with model size**. D2DMoE ([NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4c2092ec0b1370cce3fb5965ab255fae-Abstract.html), same group) gets up to 60% lower cost with dynamic k, but needs fine-tuning. The ISTA note independently arrives at the same construction item 13 already has: a transposed copy of `down_exps` for the RAM tier, so that an inactive intermediate neuron saves a whole row. In the current format, with rows of 640 in blocks of 64, a zero neuron saves nothing unless the whole block is inactive. The order of tests in item 13 is also confirmed. Berkeley's |gate| mask stays first, because it is the only one with data on Qwen MoE (84-87%) and it also saves the up rows. The per-family thresholds from the Polish paper are added in the same offline pass. The ceiling stays the one from MOD_PLAN, ~1.25x, with 10-20% realistic.

Rotation is the most concrete European lever for expert bytes, but we cannot apply it now. **QuaRot** ([arXiv 2404.00456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00456), ETH SPCL lead; EPFL, ISTA and Microsoft co) rotates the residual stream with Hadamard, the output stays unchanged, and outliers disappear: **Llama2-70B at 4 bits loses at most 0.47 perplexity on WikiText-2**. For us, the transferable part is the offline rotation folded into the weights and requantization to IQ2/IQ3 with fewer outliers. Pancake already has the runtime path: `include/strata/kernels/cpu/hadamard.hpp` applies the transform on the activation side for "Hadamard-folded" experts (the Gyro files, `prism.hadamard.*` metadata). The blocker is at the input. It would need the 354 GB of BF16, which do not fit in 78 GB, an imatrix/GSQ step on hardware we do not have, and a consistent rotation through RMSNorm, GDN, attention and the MTP head. Nobody has measured rotation combined with i-quants. Conclusion: the idea is parked, and if a rotated Flash-Next GGUF appears, pancake can run it. LQER/QERA ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/zhang24j.html), [arXiv 2410.06040](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06040v2), Imperial lead), that is, a low-rank correction of the quantization error, get the same verdict. It also needs the BF16 checkpoint and adds bytes to the VRAM cache.

The other quantization and sparsity papers do not pass the criterion. SliceGPT (ETH lead) adds losses on top of 2 bits. QUIK and Marlin are CUDA. Q-Filters (Inria ALMAnaCH, affiliation not confirmed in the listing) and TOVA (Hebrew U lead) compress a KV that in our hybrid exists only in the attention layers, and TurboQuant KV has already given zero gain. AdaSplash and ASEntmax (Lisbon) need models trained with entmax. 2:4/4:8 weight sparsity does not help on AVX2, because the metadata adds ~0.5-0.75 bpw on top of ~2.4 bpw.

## The expert cache: MoE-Infinity and SAFARI become candidate policies for item 14

**MoE-Infinity** ([arXiv 2401.14361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14361), [v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14361v3), Edinburgh lead, Luo Mai's group) is the European ancestor of pancake's per-conversation working sets (items 3-4). It builds a per-request expert activation matrix (rEAM), matches it by cosine against past requests, and evicts by predicted frequency multiplied by a bias that favors early layers, `(1 − l/L)`. It has two weaknesses. It computes nothing on the CPU, but brings every miss over PCIe. And it reports no hit rate, only latencies against weak baselines (155 ms TPOT on DeepSeek-V2-Lite against 485 ms for vLLM). An independent replication (MoE-Beyond) measured its heuristic at **17% hit with a 10% cache** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17137v1)). The eviction score is a frequency, related to ClassLRU, which already failed. Two pieces still deserve one run each in the item 14 simulator: the early-layer bias put on top of LRU, and the "nearest past conversation" predictor, which can feed the `STRATA_CONV_WORKSET` seeds. The threshold for continuing is ≥1.5 points over LRU. The successors MoE-Gen and BatchGen (OSDI 2026) optimize throughput on large batches and do not help at batch 1.

From ETH SAFARI (Mutlu) comes a family of tiny controllers learned online. **Athena** ([arXiv 2601.17615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17615v1), HPCA 2026) coordinates prefetching and miss prediction through RL and shows that a naive combination loses exactly when bandwidth is limited. **Harmonia** ([arXiv 2503.20507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20507)) separates placement from migration with two agents and reports +49.5% over the best prior method on hybrid two-device systems. For pancake, the Hermes part (miss prediction) is **redundant**: the router lookahead (10.2) already knows exactly the next layer's choices, and `STRATA_POOL_PREREAD` pre-reads them. Two ideas remain. The Harmonia split, between first-touch admission and VRAM↔RAM promotion/demotion, can be tested in the item 14 simulator: the tier loses 10 points against LRU, and admission-migration coupling is a plausible suspect. The Athena idea, a bandit over 64-token epochs that turns pre-reading on or off based on tok/s, is justified only if the A/B for `STRATA_POOL_PREREAD` comes out mixed, because pre-reading competes for the same DDR4 bandwidth. Nothing from SAFARI was evaluated on MoE expert streams. The transfer is an inference, not proof. The ceiling stays the one from the report on the rest of the US: first a time-based Belady, with the PCIe 3.0 promotion cost, and only then policies.

## The agent: type constraints, shorter thinking and an injection test

ETH SRI brings the only server idea with a probably large per-turn effect. **Type-Constrained Code Generation** ([arXiv 2504.09246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09246), [PLDI 2025](https://pldi25.sigplan.org/details/pldi-2025-papers/25/Type-Aware-Constraining-for-LLM-Code-Generation), ETH SRI lead, Berkeley co) starts from the fact that **~94% of compilation errors are type errors** and halves compilation errors by enforcing types during decoding. Pancake already guarantees syntactically valid tool calls through grammar (phase 6). The next step would be a semantic constraint on opencode's `edit` tool: `oldString` must be a substring of a text the model has already seen in the `read`/`grep` results in the conversation, enforced through a suffix automaton over those results. This would eliminate the "oldString not found" loops, which cost a whole turn plus re-prefill. Before writing code, count how often these failures appear in the opencode logs. The case where the file changed after reading must also be handled, and the mask must also be applied to MTP/lookup drafts before verification, as the grammar already does.

Also from ETH come two measurement tools. **AgentDojo** ([arXiv 2406.13352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352), ETH SPY Lab lead) has ~97 tool tasks and ~629 security cases. A single run against the opencode + pancake stack shows whether the loop guard and tool-call recovery amplify injected instructions. **SWT-Bench** ([arXiv 2406.12952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12952), ETH SRI lead) turns SWE-bench into reproducible test generation, runs locally in Docker and gives a code-specific signal, better than perplexity, for lossy decisions (Coder, `turbo`, item 13, AcceptMoE). BaxBench ([arXiv 2502.11844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11844)) covers the security side of generated code. Graph of Thoughts and KGoT (ETH SPCL lead) multiply LLM calls, and on a single-user local engine that means wall-clock time. From KGoT only the idea of keeping task state in a structured store remains, in the direction of the compaction already planned. LMQL is covered by the existing grammar, and Locally Typical Sampling by the loop guard.

From Israel come arguments for the thinking policy. **Don't Overthink it** ([arXiv 2505.17813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17813v2); Hebrew U with Meta FAIR, and Meta's role may be lead) shows that, for the same question, **the shortest sampled chain is up to 34.5% more accurate than the longest**. The authors warn that hard questions still benefit from length. This strengthens the default `low`/`medium` effort with escalation on failure, already recommended by Stanford and Berkeley. short-m@k, however, needs k parallel chains, which on an offloaded MoE at batch 1 increase the union of experts, so it is skipped. **LLMs Know More Than They Show** ([arXiv 2410.02707](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02707v4), Technion lead) shows that the truth signal concentrates on the exact answer tokens, but probes do not generalize across datasets. A probe on tool-argument tokens, trained on our logs, can use the same infrastructure as the thinking-stop probe (item 21) and can trigger the existing recovery before execution. It is a research item. AssistantBench (Tel Aviv lead) is for web agents, not coding agents.

The order follows a precise logic. P1 and P3 are small measurements that either turn a gain already obtained (`turbo`) into one usable daily, or close a hidden loss in the IQ2_XS file. P2 is the only truly new engine idea from Europe and builds directly on the `route_prior` infrastructure and on the acceptance table in `DraftPolicy`. P4 and P5 add no projects, only entries in the measurements already planned for items 13 and 14. F1 can change the daily preset more than any kernel, but only after an A/B on the same trace. M3 costs almost nothing. Explicitly skipped: GSQ/RCO requantization at home, MoESQ, Marlin/qutlass, QMoE, AQLM/PV-Tuning/HIGGS, Disaggregated Quantization, SliceGPT/QUIK, MoE-Gen/BatchGen, MoEShard, DeltaZip, the Hermes predictor (redundant with lookahead), DISCO/DSI/heterogeneous vocabularies/Jacobi, ContextPilot, DMC/DMS, BitDecoding, Q-Filters/TOVA, DeciMamba, AdaSplash/ASEntmax, Graph of Thoughts as orchestration and short-m@k.

A few gaps remain. Cambridge (Nic Lane's group), Oxford, UCL, TUM, Darmstadt, Saarland/MPI, Inria/HAL and Technion (PTQ) were not covered paper by paper. The affiliations of AcceptMoE, D2DMoE and Q-Filters are not confirmed in the PDF. No European paper evaluates cache-conditional routing or AcceptMoE on a model with 512 experts or on a DeltaNet hybrid, and the decode speed of the Coder variant is not published. The Asian offload and prediction literature (HOBBIT, ExpertFlow, Pre-gated MoE, PreScope, KTransformers) is left for the Asia report, followed by Canada and then the Pacific, Africa and Latin America.

## Conclusion

The American series looked for fewer CPU bytes in papers on sparsity and caching. Europe shows that the biggest lever is in the model file, which we choose once and then forget. The same authors who built Q2_0 also published the variant that removes half of the experts for agentic code, and the difference between them may weigh more than three rounds of cache policies. The cards also show that our own assumptions about the model (125B, "IQ2_XS", no IQ1_M) were wrong in a way that directly affects the kernels. The practical lesson: before any engine optimization, read the published allocation files as a specification.

The second change is one of perspective on lossy routing. Qualcomm and Imperial independently arrive at what pancake found empirically with `turbo`: a fine-grained MoE, with 512 experts and flat scores near the top-k threshold, tolerates picking experts from the cache. AcceptMoE adds where this is cheap to do: on draft positions that will be rejected anyway. This turns a global setting, with a uniform quality cost, into a mechanism that pays quality only where the output is not visible. If the graded prior works, the distance between the exact preset and the `turbo` one shrinks from both ends.
