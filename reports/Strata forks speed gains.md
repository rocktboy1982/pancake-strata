# Strata forks bring cheap tests, not jumps

The Strata ecosystem (about 30 forks and 11 tools, read on 2026-11-10) contains no exact kernel or trick that promises a big jump over the ~112-121 tok/s pancake already has on the RX 7900 XT. That is the main conclusion. The real value takes three forms. First: **a dozen A/B tests that need only env vars or flags**, for switches that already exist in our binary but have not been measured on gfx1100 (`STRATA_GDN_PP=2`, `STRATA_DENSE_MMQ=1`, `STRATA_PREFILL_LAYER_MAJOR=1`, `--kv-grow`, `--adapt-decay`, `--vram-reserve-mib`). Second: **a few small, exact ports**, from three sources: the prompt kernels from Strata-rdna3 that were never ported (expert GEMM -6..-9%, HC half-layer -7..-9%, measured by the author on a 7900 XTX), the prompt streaming that copies only routed experts from Strata-gfx1030 (+10% on IQ3_S, +40% on Q4_0, measured by the author on RDNA2) and the AVX2 changes for the CPU pool from Argon and Strata-GLM. The third, and the most important as a lever, is not a kernel: it is **the re-verification of fragile tokens from Strata-HotRod**. It could turn one of our already measured lossy shortcuts (turbo-hip +25-34%, tail skip ~+7%) into a safe default. Besides these there is a mandatory correction (the upstream fix #5655 for `route_prior`, which turbo-hip uses) and a rebase hazard: in v0.2.13 the hipBLASLt table is applied automatically only on gfx1151. As for our bimodal runs (~25% slower), the sources offer three verifiable mechanisms. VRAM eviction is unlikely for us, because the desktop runs on the iGPU. The remaining candidates are hugepages obtained per startup (THP `madvise`, `nr_hugepages=0`) and, specific to decode, a draft policy that "gets stuck" after a single cost outlier. Correction to a note: the card is linked at **PCIe 4.0 x16 (16 GT/s, verified in sysfs)**, not PCIe 3.0.

## Where we stand versus the ecosystem: decode leads, prompt is near parity

The starting point matters for prioritization. Pancake decodes ~112 tok/s on short chat and 118-121 tok/s with `--pcie-frac 0` (Round A, `MOD_PLAN.md`). Community reports on the RX 7900 XTX, with IQ3_S and a 24 GB card, give 77-89 tok/s on engine 0.1.59 ([PR #5695](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/pull/5695)) and 69.6 tok/s on 0.1.58 ([PR #5688](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/pull/5688)). On the same CPU as ours, an RX 6700 XT with a Ryzen 7 5700G gets 32.1 tok/s on IQ2_XS ([strata-benchmarks](https://github.com/street-tripy/strata-benchmarks/blob/main/bench.jsonl)). On decode we are therefore above every published AMD number.

On prompt, the platform note started from old figures (~800-975 tok/s) and from the assumption of a PCIe 3.0 link. To explain a ~3x gap versus the XTX, it invoked PCIe 3.0, which would have justified "at most 2x". Both premises fall. Sysfs shows `current_link_speed = 16.0 GT/s`, width 16, for `0000:12:00.0`, that is PCIe 4.0 x16. Our final suites (`bench/results/2026-11-08-7900xt-final-suites.txt`, cited in `MOD_PLAN.md` item 78) give **1,757 tok/s at 4K and 2,580 tok/s at 32K**. The XTX, with hipBLASLt 1.4.1, gives 1,484 tok/s at 4K and 3,034 at 16K ([PR #5695](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/pull/5695)). The difference is small and is explained by quantization and VRAM, not by the link. So on prompt we are not looking for a fix. We are looking for a few percent from kernels (rdna3, gfx1030) and from removing needless expert copies (layer-major, streaming only routed experts).

The current log confirms two more things. The `gfx1100-hipblaslt-100401` table is active ("hipBLASLt tuning enabled (43 rows, gfx1100, version 100401)"), although older runs in the same log show the mismatch with the 100200 file and the fallback to hipBLASEx. The VRAM reserve is the default one, 700 MiB plus 256 MiB "for what is allocated after the cache", for 10,220 slots at 15.37 GiB free (~1.43 MiB per slot). These figures set the ceiling for everything that means "VRAM recovery" below: each 256 MiB means ~180 slots, that is ~+1.8% cache capacity.

### What was rejected or does not apply, so we do not re-judge it

Several ideas from the forks duplicate measurements pancake has already closed. We list them with their status so they do not re-enter the queue. `STRATA_HC_Q8` gives +5.5-6% on sm_86 ([Speedy](https://github.com/DevDock-AI/Speedy/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)), but it is REJECTED-BEFORE for us (107.2 vs 111.7). DFlash2 reaches +49% on 2x Radeon VII, but costs -12% on a fresh 16K prompt and its acceptance is capped at ~60% by the draft model itself ([JOURNAL](https://github.com/milpster/strata-dflash2-gfx906-mixed-rocm-cuda/blob/main/JOURNAL.md)). This confirms our closure (115.9 / 84.3). The 4-bit MTP head comes out slower on a 4070 too (69.60 → 67.88, [strata-lean](https://github.com/carteakey/strata-lean)). Expert look-ahead and speculative prefetch "move the wait, they do not remove it" ([strata-q4-direct-io](https://github.com/Ribbon46/strata-q4-direct-io/blob/main/LOCAL-PORT.md)). HotRod's learned look-ahead (recall +8-13 points, decode +3%) also targets NVMe, not the CPU compute from RAM that limits us. `STRATA_GDN_LOOP` was rejected by the author himself (-10%). The two-GPU set from Speedy and gfx1030 (`--pipeline-windows 2`, `PW_RES_PATCH`, `PL_LOOKUP_STREAK`, layer split) stays parked for phase 12. The NVFP4/FP4 kernels, `GDN_TC` with tensor cores and AVX-512/AVX-VNNI need hardware we do not have.

## Correctness and rebase: two things to resolve before any lossy A/B

The first is **fix #5655**. When all 512 router logits are NaN or -inf, `route_prior_k` leaks the sentinel id `1<<30` and writes `s[33554432]` out of bounds. Under `STRATA_ROUTE_PRIOR`, this stopped serving about every 5 minutes ([PR #5655](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/pull/5655)). Speedy took it in 0.4.0 as "route-prior NaN guard" ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/DevDock-AI/Speedy/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). We found no equivalent in `src/`. The turbo-hip preset runs exactly on this path, and candidates 2.7 and 3.1 build on top of it. So the 22 lines must be applied first.

The second is **the rebase hazard from v0.2.13**. Commit `343b8eae6` makes the engine pick up the hipBLASLt table automatically only on gfx1151, and "another card needs STRATA_HIPBLASLT_TABLE=on" ([release v0.2.13](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/releases/tag/v0.2.13)). The community measured on gfx1100 an effect of 926 → 1,687 tok/s at 131K prefill on the XTX with the right table (the upstream report `2026-10-04-community-rx7900xtx-hipblaslt-100500`, indexed in [strata-benchmarks](https://github.com/street-tripy/strata-benchmarks/blob/main/bench.jsonl)), and MOD_PLAN credits our table with +38% on prompt. After the rebase, `run-pancake.sh` and the `-hip` presets must keep `STRATA_HIPBLASLT_TUNING` explicitly and add `STRATA_HIPBLASLT_TABLE=on`. The minimal check: the log shows the line "hipBLASLt tuning enabled … version 100401", not "version mismatch … using hipBLASEx". Our log contains both from different runs, so the check really catches something.

Three smaller traps are worth noting next to these. Strata-GLM found that MMQ reads past the end of matrices whose row count is not a multiple of 256, which gave random "!!!!" answers ([Strata-GLM](https://github.com/isichan-ai/Strata-GLM)). Before test 1.2 (`DENSE_MMQ`), it is worth checking whether the `% 256` gate exists in our path in `src/prefill/gemm.cu`. Upstream added and then reverted a `STRATA_MMQ_KQ` default on non-gfx12 AMD (`ffb94c578` / `88e663439`, [compare](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/compare/v0.2.12...v0.2.13)), a sign that the `MMQ_KQ=1` arm must be treated with suspicion. The strata-manager warning, "never use `pcie_frac 0` on AMD", refers to gfx1201 hanging at boot ([speed-tuning.ts](https://github.com/hongfaqiu/strata-manager/blob/main/packages/core/src/speed-tuning.ts)) and does not apply to us: `--pcie-frac 0` is measured as the best on the 7900 XT. Finally, #5531 (the arena loads based on page cache that can be freed) is worth checking once after the rebase, on the ntfs3 mount. It is a loading problem, not a speed problem.

## Bimodal runs and methodology: what the forks explain and what we adopt

No repo explicitly reports a ~25% bimodality. Still, the sources give four mechanisms that can produce on/off behavior, which we checked on our machine. **VRAM eviction** was observed on a 7900 XTX: when the desktop asked for VRAM, amdgpu evicted the expert cache (15.44 GiB) to RAM, and a 1000 MiB reserve stopped it ([strata-supervisor §13](https://github.com/sognix/strata-supervisor/blob/main/AI/IMPLEMENTATION.md)). For us it is less likely: all connectors on card1 (7900 XT) are `disconnected`, and the only connected one is `card2-HDMI-A-2`, that is the iGPU. It remains possible, though, if another ROCm process (the vision sidecar, a parallel session) allocates VRAM with only ~700 MiB of reserve. The test is cheap: `mem_info_vram_used` and `mem_info_gtt_used` recorded per run, plus `dmesg`.

**Hugepages per startup** is the best supported candidate for a bimodality that affects both decode and prompt. Our log says "MAP_HUGETLB unavailable (needed 16920 2 MiB pages, vm.nr_hugepages=0); transparent huge pages requested (MADV_HUGEPAGE)", and THP is on `madvise`. The ~33 GiB arena therefore gets 2 MiB pages only as many as the kernel manages to compact at startup. On memory fragmented after earlier runs, some startups end up on 4 KB. The 7900 GRE report describes exactly the fall to 4 KB, and upstream documents the opposite case, with minutes lost in compaction (#771, `STRATA_NO_ARENA_THP=1`, `docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md`). The test: `AnonHugePages` from `/proc/<pid>/smaps_rollup`, recorded per startup and correlated with tok/s. The A/B arms are (a) `compact_memory` before startup and (b) `STRATA_NO_ARENA_THP=1`, to force a single regime. Reserving ~34 GiB of static hugepages on 57 GB of RAM is risky, and the decision, being a system setting, belongs to the owner.

**Clock ramp**: "the first fresh prompt after loading the model is not a measurement" (`docs/AMD_HIP.md` upstream). On the XTX, the first request decoded 26% slower (59.3 vs 80.4), and the warm regime was counted only from request 4 (the report `2026-10-03-community-rx7900xtx-rocm102-nightly`). strata-manager reads `pp_dpm_sclk`/`pp_dpm_mclk` from sysfs per sample ([sysfs.rs](https://github.com/hongfaqiu/strata-manager/blob/main/crates/runtime-core/src/amd_gpu/sysfs.rs)). The same thing would show whether the slow runs sit at a lower DPM level. An experiment with `power_dpm_force_performance_level` is documented by no one (on NVIDIA, locked clocks gave -2.4% in strata3090). Being a system setting, it remains an owner decision. The fourth mechanism is specific to decode: **the draft policy gets stuck after an outlier**. A single 31 ms stall moved DraftPolicy from MTP T3 to lookup T8, and the state persisted ([gfx1030 c1af7ea34](https://github.com/jstamagal/Strata-gfx1030/commit/c1af7ea34)). An adaptation swap or a disk read can produce the same outlier for us, and the result looks exactly like a whole "slow" run. Candidate 2.6 is the direct test.

On methodology, our harness (ABBA, 95% CI, quiet gate) is stricter than any method published in the ecosystem. Comparability still needs three additions. First: **we report median [min–max] next to the CI**, as strata-manager and upstream do ([official-bench.ts](https://github.com/hongfaqiu/strata-manager/blob/main/packages/core/src/official-bench.ts)). Second: we run **the official strata-manager set**, which is `tools/strata_bench.py --runs 3 --long` (3 short questions, 4K/16K/32K prompts, a 5-turn agent session, warm-up and calibration discarded). We already have test 0.3 in `TEST_PLAN.md`, so it only needs to be fixed as the publishable reference. Third: for parameter sweeps (not for builds), we adopt the calibration model in `sweep.py`: 2 warm-up rounds, alternating ABC/CBA order, the median decides, the gain must exceed 3% and there is a final interleaved confirmation ([tuning_calibrate.rs](https://github.com/hongfaqiu/strata-manager/blob/main/crates/runtime-core/src/tuning_calibrate.rs)). From rduffyuk we take the discipline: failures are kept, nothing is silently retried, input/output/KV counters are checked independently, and a single day does not establish repeatability ([METHODS.md](https://github.com/rduffyuk/strata-context-benchmark/blob/main/studies/v7/METHODS.md)). For the quality gate of any lossy arm, the momoe protocol is the best available ([ROUTE_PRIOR.md](https://github.com/aflin/momoe/blob/main/docs/ROUTE_PRIOR.md)): KL and same top-token on ~5K mixed tokens, including an agent transcript, plus scored tasks. That is where the collapse that matters appeared: at λ1.5, tool use dropped from 95.0% to 52.5%, although the aggregate KL was only 0.251. A gate based only on token forcing would have missed it.

## Repos we take nothing from

Some repos are only rebranding or packaging: DeepStrata and Strata-NEXUS (DeepSeek V4.1, 4.7-11 tok/s), DeepSeek2333/strata-nvfp4 (a re-merge of the sergqwer fork), StrataPool (an upstream mirror without engine commits), TokenRun (settings for old PCs; DFlash2 slower than MTP) and Strata-intel-sycl-windows (SYCL packaging, upstream merges). Others target platforms with no bridge to us: dennis-akimov/Strata (Apple, Metal through ggml), strata-asahi (Vulkan; we already have `--spec-sched cost` in `draft_policy.cpp`), strata-locallane (Metal), talus-public (gfx1151 with unified memory, no CPU/PCIe tier), strata-k100ai (MMAC gfx928, ideas without per-item figures), strata-rdna4 (stagnant since 0.1.40.1, largely absorbed by upstream) and Strata-xeno (stagnant, AVX-VNNI). The ones aimed at SSD or insufficient RAM have no regime in common with us, because our arena sits entirely in RAM: strata-q4-direct-io, momoe (we keep only the quality protocol), Strata-legacy-gpu (we keep only the occupancy method and the DENSE_MMQ data). Others have only things we already have: strata-lean (ADAPT_FETCH, useless at `--pcie-frac 0`), BlockYugaLabs (CPU_SHARE auto), UnbiasedStrata (packaging without Python; the lesson about thread count is item 41) and strata-slim, from which only patch 0003 remains; patch 0002 (dense arena) is worth it only after the rounding loss in hipMalloc is measured. strateu is a Kolibri engine. From it we keep only two data points on the same 5700G CPU: the pool reaches 32.4 GB/s, and 24 threads halve decode versus 8 ([AMD_R9700_PORT.md](https://github.com/gboddaer/strateu/blob/main/docs/AMD_R9700_PORT.md)).

Among the tools, these bring no speed: strata-container, strata-docker (checked only that memlock is not limited: the log shows pin-by-need on 33,838 MiB and a successful mlock), strata-launcher, StrataSmartToggle, comfyui-strata-nodes and stratoscope (metric definitions only). strata-benchmarks is a month-old index and not a primary source. strata-supervisor contributes only the eviction observation, strata-context-benchmark only the methodology, and Strata-gfx906-Argon only the AVX2 part.

## Proposed order: groups 10-14, after specs 05-09

The test phase is now running the specs `bench/specs/05-small-flags` … `09-prefill`. We propose five new specs, in order of cost and dependencies. **10-measure** comes first, because it sets up the instruments for all the others. It contains `STRATA_DUMMY_NODES=10/40` (1.7), per-run recording of `AnonHugePages`, `mem_info_vram_used/gtt_used`, `pp_dpm_sclk/mclk` and `dmesg`, plus `strata_bench.py --runs 3 --long` as the reference with median [min–max]. It takes about an hour. **11-env-prefill** contains the env-only prompt arms: `GDN_PP=2` (1.1), `PREFILL_LAYER_MAJOR=1` (1.3), `DENSE_MMQ=1` after checking the `% 256` gate (1.2) and `--vram-reserve-mib` 444/1000 (1.6). All are measured on 2K and on 11.7K/32K. **12-env-tier** contains `--kv-grow` (1.4), the `--adapt-decay` × `--adapt-swaps` grid (1.5) and `--spec-min-p 0.7` (1.8), on all three workloads. The winners go into the preset under the "gain above the CI" rule.

**13-small-ports** needs code, in this order: fix #5655 (2.5, mandatory), PCIe staging off (2.1), the DraftPolicy clip (2.6, also tested as an explanation for the bimodality), guided pool (2.2), the accumulators and granularity from Argon (2.3), then the cherry-pick of rdna3 commits `1799c0a3a` and `3dbb40516` (3.3), with their parity benches. Each goes in as an env-toggle arm in the same binary. **14-larger** comes last and has higher stakes: first the check whether `PIN_BY_NEED` already copies only routed experts, then the `PF_STREAM_ROUTED` port (3.2), `VRAM_CALIB` (3.4) and, separately, the re-verification of fragile tokens on top of turbo-hip (3.1), with the momoe-style quality gate. `PROMPT_STAGE_MEMO` (3.5) and the dual-park disk cache (3.6) align with the branches already planned for items 39/83 and 66, not with the speed phase.

## Conclusion

The overall picture changed after this pass. Pancake is no longer a fork catching up with the ecosystem. On gfx1100 it already leads on decode and is near parity on prompt. The PCIe 4.0 correction also removes the last "hardware" explanation for a prompt gap that, in fact, does not exist. This moves the forks' value away from kernels toward two things. The first is measurement: existing switches never measured on our card, plus a set of instruments to explain the bimodal runs. The second is quality: a HotRod-style guard plus the momoe scoring protocol, which can unlock the only big gain left on the table, the +25-34% of cache-aware routing, already measured but kept opt-in.

The practical implication: the next exact percent will come from removing copies and tails (routed experts, layer-major, guided pool, staging off), each worth a few percent. The double-digit jump, if it comes, comes from making lossy safe, not from making it faster. The 10-14 order is built on this asymmetry: first the instruments and the correctness fix, the guard at the end.
